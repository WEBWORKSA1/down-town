Two contests for people who love their downtown

Downtown Lens is a photo contest for city centres in five categories. The Best Block Awards ask readers to nominate the best block in their downtown. Both are free, and both are judged on merit by a panel.

How to enter

Four steps, about five minutes

  1. Shoot

    Photograph any downtown or city centre in the world. The photo must be your own work, taken within two years before you enter.

  2. Host it yourself

    Upload the photo to your own portfolio, cloud storage or photo-sharing page, as a JPEG at least 2,000 pixels on its long edge, and make sure anyone with the link can view it.

  3. Submit

    Use the entry form: one photo per form, up to five photos per person across all categories each season.

  4. Keep the link live

    Leave the photo online until winners are announced. If the link stops working, the judges can't see your entry.

Judging criteria

What the panel looks for

Every eligible entry is scored by a judging panel of Down.Town editors and, where a category has a sponsor, one judge nominated by that sponsor. Scores are weighted like this:

Sense of place30%
Composition25%
Originality25%
Technical quality20%

Sense of place: could this only be this downtown? Composition: framing, light and timing. Originality: a fresh view of a familiar place. Technical quality: focus, exposure and editing that serves the picture.

Enter Downtown Lens

One photo per form. Up to five photos per person each season.

A link to the photo on your own portfolio, cloud storage or photo-sharing page, viewable by anyone with the link. JPEG, at least 2,000 pixels on the long edge.

Before you submit

  • The photo is yours, taken within the last two years
  • It was taken in a downtown or city centre
  • Edits are limited to normal adjustments: cropping, exposure, colour, contrast and cleaning up dust
  • No AI-generated images, composites, borders, watermarks or signatures
  • Anyone who is the main subject has agreed to be photographed and entered
  • You didn't trespass, fly a drone where it isn't allowed or break any other law to get the shot
  • The link opens for someone who isn't signed in to your account

You keep the copyright. By entering, you grant Down.Town a non-exclusive licence to display your entry, with credit, for the contest and its promotion. Details are in the official rules.

L Best Block Awards

Which block makes your downtown?

Every downtown has one: the stretch between two corners where the street feels most alive. Tell us which block it is in your city, and why. The panel reviews every nomination, and winning blocks are announced on this page and in the Dispatch, then written up by the editors.

What the panel looks for

Street life30%
Local character30%
Public space20%
Walkability20%

Street life: activity through the day and week. Local character: independent places, history and a sense that it belongs to this city. Public space: seating, shade, greenery and room to linger. Walkability: safe crossings, step-free access and transit close by.

Up to three nominations per person each season. Nominators must be 18 or older.

The rules in brief

  • Organiser: Down.Town, Montréal, Québec, Canada.
  • No purchase necessary. Both contests are free to enter. Void where prohibited or restricted by law.
  • Eligibility: individuals aged 18 or older, and at least the age of majority where they live. Down.Town editors, judges, sponsors' staff involved in the contest and their immediate families can't win.
  • Skill, not chance: winners are chosen by a judging panel on the published criteria. There is no draw and no public vote.
  • Entry limits: up to five Downtown Lens photos and three Best Block nominations per person each season.
  • Your copyright: you keep it, and grant Down.Town a non-exclusive licence to display your entry, with credit.
  • Prizes: funded by sponsors and announced on this page when sponsors confirm. No cash substitution unless stated.
  • Canadian winners must correctly answer a time-limited skill-testing question.
  • Québec residents: where required, the contest is registered with the Régie des alcools, des courses et des jeux, and a dispute may be submitted to the Régie.

This is a summary. The official rules apply, and questions can go to the contest desk.
