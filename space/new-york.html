In 30 seconds

  • Suits finance, insurance, law, media and tech firms that want a Manhattan address, harbour views and transit to New Jersey, Brooklyn and Staten Island, at less than Midtown's top prices
  • The main cores are the Financial District, the World Trade Center campus and Battery Park City
  • The quirk to know: Manhattan's Commercial Rent Tax applies below 96th Street once your rent passes a threshold

Lower Manhattan is the city's second great office market after Midtown, packed into the island's southern tip. The Financial District's older towers line Wall Street, Broad Street, Maiden Lane and Water Street on a twisting colonial street plan. To the west, the rebuilt World Trade Center campus and the towers of Battery Park City face the Hudson. The Civic Center around City Hall holds courts and government offices, the Seaport has restored low-rise buildings and piers on the East River, and Tribeca and Hudson Square to the north offer lofts that suit creative firms.

The market has been reshaped twice in a generation: first by the rebuilding after 9/11, which delivered modern towers and a vast transit hub, and more recently by a wave of office-to-residential conversions in older buildings, helped by city incentives. The result is a sharp split between new, amenity-rich towers that compete with Midtown and older buildings that either reposition, convert or offer value. Demand comes from finance, insurance, law, media, technology, government and non-profits, with the area's transit links to New Jersey and the outer boroughs a constant draw.

Retail and restaurant corridors

Fulton Street, Broadway and the shopping concourses linked to the World Trade Center transit hall carry the heaviest pedestrian traffic, driven by commuters at rush hour and lunch. The Seaport's cobbled blocks and pier rely on visitors and evening dining, and Stone Street's taverns live off after-work crowds and summer outdoor tables. Battery Park City and Tribeca serve affluent residents, which supports grocers, fitness studios, children's services and neighbourhood restaurants. The area runs on two rhythms — dense weekday office trade and tourist weekends around the Memorial and Battery Park — so many operators adjust hours and staffing between them. Restaurants applying for liquor licences go through the State Liquor Authority and usually notify the local community board first, and additional review applies where several licensed premises already sit within a short distance.

Coworking and flexible space

Lower Manhattan has one of the densest supplies of flexible workspace in the city, much of it in renovated Financial District towers and near the World Trade Center. Options range from hot desks and dedicated desks on monthly memberships to private offices and fully fitted, branded suites for teams of dozens, typically on terms of one month to two years with a deposit of one or two months' fees. Landlords also offer pre-built suites on short conventional leases. Flexible space suits start-ups, project teams, foreign companies testing New York and firms bridging the gap before a long lease.

How commercial leases work here

New York office rent is quoted per rentable square foot per year. Rentable area includes a share of lobbies and common areas, and Manhattan's loss factors are among the highest in the country, so compare the usable area you will actually occupy. Most office leases are written on a gross basis: base rent covers the landlord's real estate taxes and operating costs in a base year, and you pay your proportionate share of increases. Older leases may use a porter's wage formula for operating escalations. Electricity is usually extra, either by submeter or as a fixed charge added to rent. Retail leases are typically net, with taxes and common charges passed through.

Landlords commonly offer a work letter or tenant improvement allowance and several months of free rent, scaled to term and credit. Security is usually a cash deposit or letter of credit worth several months' rent. Small and new tenants are often asked for a "good guy" guarantee, a limited personal guarantee that releases the guarantor if the tenant gives notice, pays rent up to the date it vacates and hands back the keys. New York State does not charge sales tax on commercial rent, but the city's Commercial Rent Tax applies to tenants in Manhattan south of 96th Street above a set annual rent threshold.

Getting your team there

Few business districts anywhere match Lower Manhattan's transit. Subway lines converge at Fulton Center, the World Trade Center hub, Wall Street, Rector Street and Whitehall, linking downtown to Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx and Midtown. The PATH runs from the World Trade Center to Jersey City, Hoboken and Newark, and ferries link Battery Park City, Pier 11 and Whitehall to New Jersey, Brooklyn, Queens and Staten Island. Commuters on Metro-North and the Long Island Rail Road transfer to the subway in Midtown or Brooklyn. Driving is impractical: garages are scarce and expensive, streets are narrow, and Manhattan south of 60th Street is inside the congestion toll zone. Protected bike lanes and the Hudson River Greenway make cycling increasingly popular, so ask about bike storage and showers.

What to check before you sign

  • Commercial Rent Tax exposure, and whether your building or location qualifies for any reduction.
  • Flood risk: much of the waterfront lies in a coastal flood zone, so ask where electrical switchgear, generators and telecoms equipment sit and how the building fared in past storms.
  • Local Law 97 emissions limits for large buildings, and whether compliance upgrades or penalties could be passed through as operating costs.
  • Landmark districts around Stone Street, the Seaport and Tribeca, where the Landmarks Preservation Commission reviews storefronts, signage and façade work.
  • The certificate of occupancy for your intended use, especially for food, assembly, schools or medical.
  • Freight elevator hours, loading access and after-hours heating and cooling charges.
  • Construction next door: conversions and new towers can mean years of noise and sidewalk sheds.

Hosting events downtown

Lower Manhattan's most distinctive venues are its former banking halls, grand marble rooms now used for galas and conferences, along with rooftop terraces with harbour views and the piers and event spaces of the Seaport. Museums downtown host receptions and talks, and hotel ballrooms in the Financial District and Battery Park City suit corporate meetings. In summer, Governors Island, a short ferry ride away, hosts festivals and outdoor events. The city's main convention centre is in Midtown West, so large trade shows go there. Peak seasons are September to early December and spring, with the holiday-party season booking up months ahead. Street events need a permit from the city's street activity permit office, and parks have their own permits from the parks department or the relevant park authority.

Is downtown the right base?

Lower Manhattan suits firms that want a Manhattan address with unmatched transit to New Jersey, Brooklyn and Staten Island, harbour views and a growing residential neighbourhood around the office, often at less than Midtown's top rents. Midtown remains the deeper market for headquarters, banking and law, with Grand Central and Penn Station for suburban commuters, while Midtown South, around Union Square and Chelsea, leads for tech and creative firms. Downtown Brooklyn and the Jersey City waterfront offer value across the water. For the downtown neighbourhood beyond the office, see the downtown New York guide.
