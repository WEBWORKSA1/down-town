In 30 seconds

  • Who it suits: financial, professional-services and technology firms that want Australia's deepest pool of talent and clients
  • The main office cores: Martin Place and the northern core, Barangaroo and the western corridor, with Midtown and the southern CBD as value options
  • The one lease quirk to know: rent is usually quoted net, so outgoings and GST go on top — and the incentive package matters as much as the face rent

Sydney's CBD runs south from Circular Quay to Central Station. The northern core — Macquarie, Phillip and Bligh Streets and Martin Place — has the harbour views and the prestige addresses. Barangaroo and the western corridor along Kent, Clarence and Sussex Streets have the newest and largest floors. The middle of the CBD around Town Hall and Park Street, often called Midtown, mixes older towers with the main retail precinct, and the southern CBD around Central and Haymarket is being reshaped as a technology precinct. Beyond the CBD, Pyrmont and Ultimo hold media and tech offices, Surry Hills has creative studios, and North Sydney and Parramatta function as separate business centres.

This is Australia's largest office market and home to many of its biggest banks, insurers, fund managers, law firms and consultancies, along with the regional headquarters of global companies. Building stock ranges from heritage sandstone to brand-new towers, and the gap between them has widened: since the pandemic, tenants have gravitated toward newer, better-located buildings with strong energy ratings and end-of-trip facilities, while older stock competes on price and incentives. The metro beneath the CBD has given buildings near its stations a lift. Office attendance is noticeably stronger in the middle of the week, which shapes both how much space firms take and how CBD shops and cafés trade.

Where the offices are

Northern core and Martin PlaceThe prestige end of the CBD between Circular Quay and Martin Place, home to the Reserve Bank of Australia, major banks and top-tier law firms in a mix of trophy towers and older premium buildings. Suits firms where address and harbour views matter.
Barangaroo and the western corridorLarge, modern floors on the former wharves and along Kent, Clarence and Sussex Streets, near Barangaroo metro and Wynyard station. Suits big tenants consolidating onto efficient floors with strong sustainability credentials.
MidtownThe centre of the CBD around Town Hall, Park Street and Pitt Street Mall, with a wide range of building ages above the retail core and Gadigal and Town Hall stations below. Suits mid-size firms that want a central location for less than the northern core.
Southern CBD and Tech CentralThe area around Central Station and Haymarket, where the NSW Government is developing a technology and innovation precinct. Converted warehouses and new towers suit startups, tech companies and education providers, with the best rail connections in the city.
North SydneyA separate office market across the Harbour Bridge with its own metro station, popular with technology, telecommunications and insurance firms that want lower rents and easy access to the North Shore suburbs.

Retail and restaurant corridors

The CBD's retail spine is Pitt Street Mall and the pedestrianised stretch of George Street, anchored by department stores, the Queen Victoria Building and a chain of arcades and malls linked underground. Luxury brands cluster around Castlereagh and King Streets. Circular Quay and The Rocks trade on visitors, with steady footfall seven days a week and peaks on cruise-ship days and during big harbour events, while Barangaroo and Darling Harbour rely on a mix of office workers, tourists and convention crowds. Chinatown and Haymarket support affordable dining late into the evening.

Elsewhere in the core, cafés and food courts live and die by office workers: Tuesday to Thursday are the strongest days, Mondays and Fridays are softer, and weekends are quiet away from the shopping and tourist areas. The laneways around Angel Place, Ash Street and Bridge Lane, and the small-bar scene that followed licensing reforms, give independent operators a way in without main-street rents. Change of use, outdoor dining and signage generally need approval from the City of Sydney, and alcohol needs a licence from Liquor & Gaming NSW.

Coworking and flexible space

Flexible workspace is a big part of the Sydney market. Operators cluster in the northern core and around Martin Place, in Barangaroo and the Midtown towers, and in Surry Hills, Pyrmont and North Sydney. Options run from hot desks and dedicated desks on monthly memberships to private offices for teams on terms of a few months to a couple of years. Landlords have also moved in with fitted, ready-to-occupy suites on two- to three-year leases, which sit between coworking and a traditional lease and avoid the capital cost of a fit-out. Flexible space suits overseas companies testing Australia, project teams and small professional firms; once you are stable, a conventional lease with a fit-out incentive usually offers better value.

How commercial leases work here

Australian office rents are quoted per square metre per year, either net or gross. On a net lease, you pay the base rent plus your share of the building's outgoings — council rates, water, insurance, cleaning, air-conditioning maintenance, management and, for most office leases, land tax — apportioned by your share of the lettable area. On a gross lease, outgoings are built into the rent, although increases above a base year may still be passed on. GST is charged on rent and outgoings. Leases usually include annual rent reviews, either a fixed percentage or a CPI adjustment, with market reviews when options are exercised. Landlords commonly offer incentives — rent-free months or a fit-out contribution — which can be substantial, so compare effective rents rather than face rents.

Security is usually a bank guarantee, commonly sized at several months' gross rent including GST. At expiry, a make-good clause typically requires you to remove your fit-out and return the space to its original condition. Shops, restaurants and other retail premises fall under the Retail Leases Act 1994 in New South Wales, which requires the landlord to give a disclosure statement, sets a five-year minimum term unless the tenant waives it with a solicitor's certificate, and limits which costs can be passed on; most office leases fall outside the Act. Leases of more than three years should be registered on the title to protect your interest if the building is sold.

Getting your team there

The CBD has the best public-transport access in Australia. The City Circle stations — Central, Town Hall, Wynyard, Circular Quay, St James and Museum — serve the main suburban lines, and the metro stops at Barangaroo, Martin Place, Gadigal and Central, linking the north-west and the lower North Shore with the city. Light rail runs the length of George Street, ferries arrive at Circular Quay and Barangaroo, and buses fill in from the eastern suburbs and the Northern Beaches. A network of separated cycleways reaches the core, and end-of-trip facilities are a selling point in newer towers. Parking is limited and expensive, and tolls on the harbour crossings add to the cost of driving, so few staff drive in daily. Buildings near Wynyard, Martin Place and Central have the broadest commuter catchment, and Barangaroo has become far easier to reach since the metro opened.

What to check before you sign

  • Outgoings budget. Ask for the current outgoings budget and recent actuals, and check exactly which items are recoverable.
  • Effective rent and incentives. Compare incentive packages across buildings and check whether they come as rent-free time, a fit-out contribution or a cash payment.
  • NABERS rating. Check the energy rating on the building's disclosure certificate; it affects running costs and many corporate sustainability commitments.
  • Make-good scope. Agree precisely what must be removed at the end, and whether a cash payment can replace physical make-good.
  • Bank guarantee terms. Confirm the amount, whether it steps down over time and how quickly it is returned after you leave.
  • Heritage and fire safety. Heritage-listed sandstone buildings limit what you can change; for any building, ask for the latest annual fire safety statement.
  • Retail approvals. For shops and restaurants, check the disclosure statement, approved use, outdoor-dining eligibility and kitchen exhaust routes before you sign.

Hosting events downtown

The International Convention Centre at Darling Harbour is the city's main venue for conferences, exhibitions and large gala dinners, with the surrounding hotels covering overflow and smaller meetings. For a sense of place, the Opera House, the Museum of Contemporary Art, the Art Gallery of New South Wales, Customs House and the heritage buildings of The Rocks all hire out function spaces, while harbour cruises, wharf venues and the Royal Botanic Garden suit receptions and launches. Rooftop bars and laneway venues handle smaller groups. Spring and autumn are the most popular seasons; demand spikes around Vivid Sydney and New Year's Eve, when harbour-view venues book out far ahead and road closures complicate logistics. Events in public spaces need permits — from the City of Sydney for streets and parks, and from Place Management NSW for the foreshore precincts it looks after, such as The Rocks, Darling Harbour and Barangaroo.

Is downtown the right base?

For firms that recruit from across the whole metropolitan area, meet clients in person and want to be inside the city's financial and legal ecosystem, the CBD remains the obvious choice. But Sydney is a multi-centre city. North Sydney offers lower rents and its own metro station, Parramatta sits at the heart of Western Sydney's growing workforce, Macquarie Park suits technology and pharmaceutical firms that want large floors and parking, and Pyrmont, Ultimo and Surry Hills offer creative, low-rise space close to the centre. Many firms now pair a smaller CBD office with flexible space elsewhere. Read the downtown Sydney guide to see what the CBD offers your team beyond the office.
