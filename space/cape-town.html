In 30 seconds

  • Who it suits: tech and digital teams, financial services, film and creative firms, customer-service operations and hospitality
  • The main office cores: the CBD and Foreshore, the V&A Waterfront, and Woodstock and Salt River
  • The one lease quirk to know: rent rises by a fixed annual escalation, with operating costs and rates recovered on top

Downtown Cape Town is squeezed between Table Mountain and the sea, which gives it a compact commercial core. The CBD's historic grid holds older office buildings, banks and government departments around Adderley Street and St George's Mall, while the Foreshore, built on reclaimed land, adds post-war and modern towers near the convention centre and the main station. The V&A Waterfront is a managed precinct of offices, retail and hotels around a working harbour, and the inner suburbs of Woodstock and Salt River have turned warehouses into studios.

The market leans on financial services, technology, film and advertising production, tourism and a sizeable customer-service and back-office sector serving international clients. Many older CBD buildings have been converted to apartments, bringing more residents and evening life downtown. Tenants increasingly prioritise buildings with backup power, water resilience, security and parking, and well-managed modern space commands a clear premium over older stock.

Retail and restaurant corridors

Bree Street is the CBD's restaurant, café and boutique strip, busy at lunch with office workers and in the evenings with diners. Long Street mixes bars, clubs and backpacker trade with heritage buildings. Kloof Street in Gardens is a neighbourhood strip of restaurants and shops under the mountain, while St George's Mall and Adderley Street serve commuters and everyday shoppers. The V&A Waterfront draws the biggest visitor numbers, all week and especially at weekends, within a single managed precinct. Footfall is strongly seasonal: the summer tourist season from December to March is the peak, and winter is quieter. Concepts that serve both locals and visitors tend to ride out the seasons best.

Coworking and flexible space

Cape Town's flexible-office market has grown with its tech sector and its popularity with remote workers. Operators cluster in the CBD, around Bree Street, in Woodstock, at the Waterfront and in Gardens. Expect monthly memberships for hot desks and dedicated desks, private offices for small teams and meeting rooms by the hour. Backup power, fast fibre and secure parking are the main selling points, so check each. Flexible space suits international companies testing the market, project teams and start-ups that may grow quickly.

How commercial leases work here

South African commercial leases are largely a matter of contract between landlord and tenant. Office leases commonly run three to five years, sometimes longer for anchor tenants, and include a fixed annual escalation rather than an index-linked review. Rent is quoted per square metre per month, either gross or as a net figure plus recoveries: a share of the building's operating costs, municipal rates and, increasingly, the cost of backup power and security. Electricity and water are usually sub-metered and recharged. VAT is charged on commercial rent and recoveries.

Landlords commonly ask for a deposit or bank guarantee and, for smaller companies, personal suretyships from the directors, so read those carefully. Tenant installation allowances are negotiated on larger deals, and leases usually require the tenant to reinstate the premises at the end. Leases of ten years or more should be registered against the property's title to fully protect the tenant if the building is sold.

Getting your team there

Most office workers drive or use minibus taxis, so parking is a critical part of any lease negotiation; ask how many bays come with the space and what extra bays cost. The MyCiTi bus network connects the CBD with the Waterfront, the Atlantic seaboard, the West Coast suburbs and the airport, and its main station is beside the Foreshore. Metrorail commuter trains run into Cape Town station, but services have been unreliable, so few employers rely on them. Ride-hailing is widely used for meetings and late finishes. Rush-hour traffic on the main highways into town is heavy, which favours flexible start times.

What to check before you sign

  • Ask what backup power the building has, how much of your load it covers and how its costs are recovered.
  • Check water resilience, such as storage tanks, a lasting concern since the drought that hit the city in 2018.
  • Confirm parking allocations, visitor parking and access control in writing.
  • Check that the zoning permits your use, and whether heritage approval is needed for alterations to older buildings.
  • Review the escalation rate, the recovery schedule and what counts as an operating cost.
  • Read any suretyship carefully, and understand how much personal liability it creates.
  • For restaurants and bars, factor in the liquor licence, signage approval, extraction and late-night noise rules.

Hosting events downtown

The Cape Town International Convention Centre on the Foreshore is the anchor for large conferences and exhibitions, including the Investing in African Mining Indaba each February. Downtown also offers hotel ballrooms, the historic City Hall and other heritage buildings, galleries, rooftops with mountain views and Waterfront venues by the water, with wine estates within an hour's drive. The summer season is peak time, but the south-easter can disrupt outdoor events, and winter calls for indoor or covered plans. Events in public spaces need a permit from the city, applied for well in advance.

Is downtown the right base?

For client-facing firms, tourism businesses and teams that want to be central, the CBD, Foreshore and Waterfront are the natural choice, and Woodstock suits creative and tech companies looking for space and character. Many larger employers, however, choose Century City in the north, Claremont in the southern suburbs or Bellville for purpose-built offices, easier parking and shorter commutes for staff.
