In 30 seconds

  • Who it suits: consultancies, associations, law and lobbying firms, IT contractors and NGOs that need to be near Parliament and federal departments
  • The main office cores: the Central Business District south of Parliament Hill and the federal complexes of Gatineau's Hull sector, with the ByWard Market for smaller firms
  • The one lease quirk to know: one region, two provinces — an Ottawa lease follows Ontario rules with 13% HST, while a Gatineau lease falls under Quebec's Civil Code, often in French, with GST plus QST

Ottawa's office core is the Central Business District, a grid of towers south of Parliament Hill along Albert, Slater, Queen and Laurier streets, served by the O-Train tunnel's Lyon and Parliament stations. Wellington and Sparks streets, at the edge of the Parliamentary Precinct, hold heritage buildings and mid-rise offices favoured by associations and lobbyists. East of the Rideau Canal, the ByWard Market and Lowertown offer low-rise space for smaller firms; to the south, Centretown mixes converted houses with mid-rise offices; and to the west, LeBreton Flats and Bayview are redevelopment districts with an innovation centre at their heart. Across the Ottawa River, Gatineau's Hull sector has a downtown of its own, built around large federal office complexes.

The federal government is the defining tenant. Departments and agencies occupy much of the space in the core and in Gatineau, where the government has long placed a share of its offices, and around them cluster consultancies, IT and defence contractors, law firms, associations, unions and NGOs. Much of the stock dates from the 1960s to the 1980s, and building heights in the core are limited to protect views of the Parliament Buildings. With federal hybrid-work rules and plans to shrink the government's office portfolio, availability has risen, especially in older buildings, while modern, efficient space near transit remains in demand. Private tenants have more choice than in years — and more reason to look closely at what their neighbours plan to do.

Retail and restaurant corridors

Rideau Street and the large shopping centre at Rideau station are the main regional shopping destination downtown, busy every day of the week. The ByWard Market combines food shops, seasonal stalls, restaurants and the city's densest nightlife, peaking in the evenings, at weekends and through the summer tourist season. Sparks Street, a pedestrian mall a block south of Parliament, lives on office workers and visitors, so its trade follows federal office hours — midweek is busiest, Mondays and Fridays quieter. Elgin Street, rebuilt with wider sidewalks, is a restaurant and pub strip; Bank Street runs through Centretown and the Glebe with neighbourhood retail; and Preston Street's Little Italy and Wellington West draw diners and independent shoppers. In Gatineau, the streets around the Promenade du Portage serve federal workers by day and a later-night bar crowd. Big events — Winterlude, the Tulip Festival, Canada Day and Bluesfest — bring sharp spikes in footfall, and the ByWard Market is a heritage conservation district with rules on storefronts and signs.

Coworking and flexible space

Flexible workspace is spread through the Central Business District, the ByWard Market and the neighbourhoods just west of downtown, with a City-backed innovation centre at Bayview Yards offering programmes and space for start-ups. Typical terms are monthly memberships for hot desks and dedicated desks, and private offices for small teams on terms from a few months to a year, with internet and meeting rooms included. Flex space suits consultants and out-of-town firms that need a downtown base near federal clients, and teams testing Ottawa before signing a lease. It is rarely suitable for work involving protected or classified government information, which usually needs controlled premises.

How commercial leases work here

On the Ontario side, commercial leases follow the same conventions as elsewhere in the province, with the Commercial Tenancies Act in the background. Most are net: base rent per square foot per year on rentable area, plus additional rent for operating costs and realty taxes, often called TMI, estimated monthly and reconciled annually, with 13% HST on top. Ontario landlords have strong remedies, including the right to change the locks once rent is more than 15 days in arrears, and personal guarantees are common for small firms. Negotiation focuses on free rent, tenant-improvement allowances, renewal and early-termination rights, and caps on controllable costs.

In Gatineau, leases fall under the Civil Code of Québec. They are often drafted in French — under the Charter of the French Language, standard-form contracts must first be offered in French — and rent carries 5% GST plus 9.975% QST. The Code's tacit-renewal rule and the value of publishing the lease at the land register are worth raising with your lawyer. Payroll and employment rules generally follow the province where your staff report to work, so the river crossing matters for more than the lease.

Getting your team there

OC Transpo's O-Train Line 1 runs east–west in a tunnel under downtown, with Lyon and Parliament stations in the heart of the Central Business District and Rideau station at the ByWard Market; Line 2 runs north–south from Bayview, and a short airport line connects with it. Buses fill the gaps, and staff can pay with Presto or a tapped credit or debit card. From Gatineau, the STO's buses, including the Rapibus corridor, and several bridges bring commuters into the core, and many people walk or cycle across. The National Capital Commission's pathways along the canal and rivers, plus protected bike lanes such as Laurier Avenue's, make cycling practical for much of the year. Parking in the core is limited, and the Queensway is congested at peak hours. VIA Rail links Ottawa with Montréal and Toronto for day trips. The blocks around Lyon and Parliament stations are the easiest to reach from both sides of the river.

What to check before you sign

  • Security requirements: if you will bid on federal contracts involving protected or classified information, your premises may need to meet the federal Contract Security Program's standards — check access control and whether secure rooms can be built.
  • Which side of the river: Ontario or Quebec law, taxes, language rules and payroll obligations all follow the address.
  • Heritage and height limits: protected views of Parliament, heritage designations on Sparks Street and the ByWard Market conservation district limit alterations and signage.
  • Neighbouring federal plans: ask whether nearby government buildings are being vacated, consolidated or redeveloped, which can change footfall and the feel of the block.
  • Operating costs and taxes: request several years of figures and how capital repairs and snow removal are charged.
  • After-hours HVAC and building hours: confirm charges and access for evening and weekend work.
  • Demonstrations and closures: near Parliament, protests and security perimeters can close streets and complicate deliveries.

Hosting events downtown

Ottawa's convention centre, beside the Rideau Canal and the Rideau shopping district, handles national conferences and association meetings, and hotel ballrooms across the core cover galas and receptions. The national museums and galleries, the National Arts Centre and the historic exhibition halls at Lansdowne add memorable settings, and Gatineau's venues include the Canadian Museum of History with its views of Parliament. The busiest seasons are spring and autumn, when Parliament sits and associations come to town, along with the festival peaks of Winterlude, the Tulip Festival and Canada Day. Events on City property need a City of Ottawa special event permit, events on federal land such as Major's Hill Park or along Confederation Boulevard need National Capital Commission approval, and alcohol at a one-off event requires a special occasion permit from the AGCO in Ontario or a permit from the Régie des alcools, des courses et des jeux in Gatineau.

Is downtown the right base?

If your clients are federal departments, parliamentarians or national associations, downtown Ottawa — or the Hull sector across the river — is where you want to be, and the current market offers more choice than it has for years. Other districts serve particular needs: Kanata North in the west end is the region's technology park, the federal campus at Tunney's Pasture and the redeveloping LeBreton Flats sit along the O-Train to the west, and the business parks in the south and east suit firms whose staff drive. Gatineau can lower occupancy costs, but compare the full picture of Quebec law, language and tax first. For the wider picture, see the downtown Ottawa guide.
