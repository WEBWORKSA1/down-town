In 30 seconds

  • Who it suits: headquarters, foreign subsidiaries, professional firms and anyone who needs to be near government
  • The main office cores: Gwanghwamun, Jongno, City Hall and Euljiro, and Seoul Station
  • The one lease quirk to know: a large refundable deposit sits on top of monthly rent and a management fee, all quoted per pyeong

Downtown Seoul's commercial core sits inside the line of the old city walls. Its northern edge is Gwanghwamun, where Sejong-daero runs down from the palace past ministries and embassies; its southern edge is Seoul Station, the national rail hub. Between them lie the office towers of Jongno and the Cheonggyecheon stream, the banking blocks around City Hall and Namdaemun-ro, the shopping streets of Myeong-dong and the wholesale markets of Namdaemun and Dongdaemun. Locals call this the CBD, one of three big office cores alongside Gangnam and Yeouido.

The CBD is the most established and prestigious of the three. Tenants include conglomerate headquarters, banks and insurers, law and accounting firms, consultancies, media groups, public institutions and the Korean offices of multinationals. The building stock runs from ageing mid-rise blocks to large, modern Grade A towers, and older districts such as Euljiro and the area around Seoul Station are steadily being rebuilt through urban redevelopment. Demand rests on prestige, proximity to government and a transit network that reaches the centre from every direction.

Where the offices are

Gwanghwamun and Sejong-daeroThe capital's ceremonial address: government complexes, embassies, major newspapers, conglomerates and foreign companies in large towers. Served by Gwanghwamun (Line 5) and nearby Line 1 and 3 stations; best for firms that value prestige and access to ministries.
Jongno and CheonggyecheonTowers lining the stream and the old Jongno avenue, with a mix of modern Grade A and older mid-rise stock. Line 1 runs underneath; a good choice for mid-sized firms wanting a central address at less than Gwanghwamun's premium.
City Hall, Euljiro and Myeong-dongBanks, insurers and financial institutions around Seoul Plaza and Namdaemun-ro, with redevelopment replacing old workshop blocks along Euljiro. Line 2 links it directly to Gangnam; suits finance and professional services.
Seoul Station and Namdaemun-roNewer large-floor towers next to the high-speed rail terminus and the airport railway. Ideal for companies with staff travelling nationwide or clients arriving from Incheon.
Dongdaemun and eastern EuljiroSmaller and older buildings near the fashion markets and the Dongdaemun Design Plaza, attracting design studios, fashion businesses, trading firms and cost-conscious startups.

Retail and restaurant corridors

Myeong-dong is the city's most intense shopping street, dominated by cosmetics, fashion and street-food stalls; footfall depends heavily on international visitors and peaks in the evening and at weekends. Nearby, department-store flagships anchor the area around Hoehyeon and Namdaemun-ro. Gwanghwamun, Jongno and City Hall live on weekday office trade: packed lunch counters and after-work restaurants and pubs, much quieter at weekends except near the palaces. Insadong draws weekend crowds for crafts, galleries and tea houses, and as a designated culture district it has rules that favour traditional businesses. Ikseon-dong's hanok and Euljiro's workshop buildings have become magnets for small, design-led cafés and bars, with tiny footprints and steep stairs. Long underground arcades beneath Euljiro, City Hall and Myeong-dong host small shops serving commuters, while Namdaemun and Dongdaemun markets trade wholesale, some through the night.

Coworking and flexible space

Flexible workspace is well established in the CBD, with operators taking whole floors in towers around Gwanghwamun, City Hall, Jongno and Seoul Station. Options range from hot desks and dedicated desks to lockable private offices for small teams, usually on monthly or short-term agreements with furniture, internet, meeting rooms and reception included. Serviced offices are a common first base for foreign companies opening a branch or liaison office while they recruit and search for a direct lease. If you plan to use the address for business registration, confirm in writing that the operator supports it, as the tax office may ask for evidence of a genuine place of business.

How commercial leases work here

Korean commercial leases combine three payments: a refundable deposit (bojeunggeum), monthly rent and a separate monthly management fee (gwallibi). Rent and management fees are quoted per pyeong, a traditional unit of about 3.3 square metres, usually on contract area, which includes a share of lobbies, corridors and plant. Because the ratio of exclusive (usable) area to contract area varies widely between buildings, compare options on the cost per pyeong of exclusive area, folding in rent-free months and management fees. Jeonse, the deposit-only arrangement famous in housing, is rarely used for offices.

The Commercial Building Lease Protection Act gives qualifying tenants a right to request renewal for a cumulative period, currently up to ten years, which the landlord may refuse only on grounds set out in the law, such as serious rent arrears. It also limits increases on renewal for leases within certain deposit thresholds and protects a retail tenant's opportunity to recover key money (gwonrigeum) from an incoming tenant. To protect your deposit, register your business at the premises and obtain a fixed-date confirmation on the lease from the tax office. At the end of the term, tenants are normally expected to restore the space to its original condition (wonsang bokgu).

Conventions vary by building and landlord. Treat this as orientation, not legal advice, and have a local lawyer review any lease.

Getting your team there

Almost every CBD building is within a few minutes' walk of the subway. Lines 1 and 2 meet at City Hall, Line 5 serves Gwanghwamun and Euljiro, Line 4 runs through Myeong-dong to Seoul Station, and Line 3 skirts the palaces. Seoul Station adds high-speed KTX trains, the airport railway to Incheon and Gimpo, and new express commuter rail opening in stages. Central bus lanes along Jongno and Sejong-daero bring in city buses, and red express buses connect the satellite cities of Gyeonggi Province. Line 2 gives a direct run to Gangnam's business district, and Line 5 reaches Yeouido. Parking is the weak point: towers allocate a limited number of spaces in proportion to leased area, usually charged monthly, so most staff commute by transit and visitors should be pointed to the nearest station exit.

What to check before you sign

  • Pull the property register to see mortgages and other claims ranking ahead of your deposit, and ask your lawyer whether to register your lease or seek a deposit guarantee.
  • Check the building ledger: the registered use must suit your business, and unauthorised alterations can block business permits, especially for restaurants and clinics.
  • Measure the exclusive area and calculate the efficiency ratio rather than relying on the quoted contract area.
  • Ask exactly what the management fee covers and how after-hours and weekend air conditioning and heating are charged.
  • Confirm your parking allocation and visitor parking arrangements in the lease.
  • Document the handover condition with photos and drawings so the restoration obligation at exit is clear.
  • For shops, ask about key money, signage permits from the district office, culture-district rules in Insadong and any redevelopment plans affecting the block.

Hosting events downtown

Downtown offers a wide range of venues. Dongdaemun Design Plaza hosts exhibitions, fashion shows and product launches in its sweeping halls; the Sejong Center for the Performing Arts on Sejong-daero suits concerts and formal gatherings; and hotel ballrooms around City Hall, Gwanghwamun and Namsan handle conferences and banquets. Rooftop terraces, galleries and restored hanok in Bukchon and Seochon work well for intimate dinners and receptions. Seoul Plaza and Gwanghwamun Square host public events, but they require permits from the city and are booked well in advance. The largest trade shows go to convention centres in Gangnam and in Goyang, north-west of the city. Spring and autumn are peak seasons, December fills with year-end company parties, and the Seollal and Chuseok holidays are best avoided.

Is downtown the right base?

Choose the CBD if you want a prestigious address, proximity to government and diplomatic missions, and the best transit links in the country, including direct rail from both airports. Gangnam's business district, along Teheran-ro, suits technology firms, startups, consumer brands and companies recruiting younger staff who live south of the river. Yeouido remains the home of securities houses, asset managers and broadcasters, and Seongsu and the Pangyo tech valley attract creative and technology businesses at lower cost. Many international firms start in a serviced office downtown and decide once they know where their clients and recruits are. For the wider picture, read the downtown Seoul guide.
