In 30 seconds

  • Who it suits: finance, professional services, trading and regional headquarters serving the Middle East, Africa and South Asia
  • The main office cores: DIFC, Business Bay, the Burj Khalifa district, Sheikh Zayed Road and the Trade Centre, plus Deira and Bur Dubai
  • The one lease quirk to know: your office, lease registration, trade licence and visa allowance are tied together, so choose them as one decision

Dubai's modern business centre stretches along Sheikh Zayed Road, from the Trade Centre area past the financial free zone to the towers around the Burj Khalifa and Business Bay. The older commercial heart lies along Dubai Creek in Deira and Bur Dubai, where trading houses, banks and family businesses have worked for generations. Beyond the centre, a string of free zones further along the coast host media, technology and commodity firms.

The market is shaped by the city's role as a regional hub: banks and funds in the financial free zone, multinationals running Middle East and Africa operations, traders and logistics firms, and a fast-growing community of founders and family offices. Building stock is mostly recent, with many towers in strata ownership, meaning floors are owned by different investors. Demand for high-quality, well-managed space has been strong, which tends to favour landlords, while older and strata-owned buildings vary widely in quality.

Where the offices are

DIFCThe financial free zone, with its own common-law courts and financial regulator. Banks, funds, law firms and family offices in premium towers around the zone's landmark gate building, on the Red Line.
Business BayDozens of mainland towers along the canal extension, from fitted small units to full floors, often in strata buildings. Good value for growing firms; check building quality closely.
Burj Khalifa districtMixed-use towers and boulevard offices next to the city's flagship attractions, popular with real-estate, hospitality and consumer brands.
Sheikh Zayed Road and Trade CentreThe original skyscraper strip, with the exhibition centre and government-linked towers. Central, with direct metro access and a mix of older and refurbished buildings.
Deira and Bur DubaiTraditional office buildings near the Creek, the souks and the airport. Lower costs and a strong base for trading, shipping and import businesses.

Retail and restaurant corridors

Retail in Dubai is dominated by large malls, where operators control the tenant mix, fit-out standards and opening hours, and leases may include turnover rent. Outside the malls, the boulevard around the Burj Khalifa and the financial free zone's podiums suit restaurants and premium brands. Street-front retail thrives in older districts — the gold and textile souks, Karama, Al Rigga and the shopping streets of Bur Dubai and Deira — where independent shops and eateries serve residents and bargain-hunting visitors. Footfall peaks in the cooler months from October to April and during the shopping festival, falls outdoors in summer and shifts to evenings and late nights during Ramadan. Weekends are the busiest days, and evenings are busier than mornings year-round.

Coworking and flexible space

Flexible space is a big part of the Dubai market because it can solve a licensing problem as well as a space problem. Business centres and coworking floors are common in DIFC, Business Bay and the Trade Centre area, offering hot desks, dedicated desks and serviced private offices on monthly or annual terms. Many free zones sell flexi-desk packages that come bundled with a licence and a small visa allocation. Before you sign, confirm that the agreement is accepted by your licensing authority, how many employee visas it supports and whether it includes a registered address.

How commercial leases work here

On the Dubai mainland, landlord–tenant relations are governed by the emirate's tenancy law, and every lease must be registered through Ejari, the Dubai Land Department's registration system. Ejari registration is needed for a trade licence, utility connections and visa processing. Rent increases on renewal are tied to the official rental index, landlords must give advance notice of changes, and disputes go to a dedicated rental disputes centre. Free zones set their own rules and register leases through their own authorities, and DIFC has its own leasing law and registry.

Rent is usually quoted per square foot per year, often inclusive of the building's service charge, though district cooling charges may be billed separately. It has traditionally been paid in advance by one to four post-dated cheques, with electronic payment increasingly accepted. Leases often run for one to three years with annual renewals. Space ranges from shell and core to fully fitted, and landlords commonly grant a rent-free period for fit-out. Fit-out works need landlord consent and approval from the relevant authority.

Getting your team there

The Metro's Red Line runs the length of Sheikh Zayed Road, with stations serving the Trade Centre area, DIFC, the Burj Khalifa district and Business Bay, and continues to the airport. The Green Line serves Deira and Bur Dubai. Air-conditioned walkways link several stations to nearby towers, but in summer even short walks are hard work, so many staff drive or use taxis and ride-hailing. Parking is a major consideration: check how many bays come with the lease and the cost of extra bays, and factor in road tolls and paid street parking. Rush-hour traffic on Sheikh Zayed Road and the Creek crossings is heavy, which makes a metro-side building a real advantage.

What to check before you sign

  • Decide between mainland and free zone first, because the choice determines your licence, your office options and where you can trade.
  • Confirm that the space size supports the number of employee visas you need.
  • Check that the lease can be registered through Ejari, or with the relevant free-zone authority or DIFC, before paying.
  • Clarify what the rent includes: service charge, district cooling, parking and utilities.
  • In strata towers, ask about building management, maintenance quality and lift capacity at peak times.
  • Review renewal terms, notice periods and the grounds on which the landlord can end the lease.
  • Get written approval for fit-out works and agree the reinstatement standard at the end.

Hosting events downtown

Dubai is a year-round events city with a winter peak. The exhibition centre at the Trade Centre hosts large trade shows and conferences, and the exhibition centre at Expo City handles more on the city's southern edge. Downtown venues include hotel ballrooms, the opera house, galleries in the financial free zone, rooftop terraces with views of the Burj Khalifa, heritage houses in Al Fahidi and boats on the Creek. October to April is the season for outdoor events; summer pushes almost everything indoors. During Ramadan, schedules shift to evenings and iftar gatherings. Public events need permits from the relevant government departments, so allow time for approvals.

Is downtown the right base?

For finance and firms that want to be seen at the centre of the market, DIFC and the Burj Khalifa district are the obvious choices, and Business Bay offers more space for the money nearby. Trading and import businesses often do better in Deira and Bur Dubai, close to the Creek and the airport. Technology, media and commodity firms frequently choose the free zones further along the coast for their licensing packages and campus-style offices.
