In 30 seconds

  • Suits corporate regional offices, tech and fintech firms, logistics, media and film companies that want Sun Belt costs and the world-class airport
  • The main cores are Downtown, Midtown and Buckhead, strung along Peachtree Street and MARTA's north–south line
  • The quirk to know: parking is a line item — check the parking ratio and monthly charges alongside the rent

Atlanta's commercial centre is a corridor rather than a single core. Downtown, around Five Points and Centennial Olympic Park, holds the state government, city hall, federal offices, Georgia State University and one of the country's largest convention districts. Midtown, a couple of miles north, has become the city's most active office market, with newer towers along Peachtree and West Peachtree streets and Georgia Tech's Tech Square at its western edge. Buckhead, further north, mixes towers with luxury retail and residential high-rises. Beyond the city, suburban office parks at Perimeter Center, Cumberland and Alpharetta compete for corporate tenants.

The market is driven by corporate relocations and expansions, the airport's global reach, a deep university talent pipeline and a cost base that remains below the big coastal cities. Midtown captured most of the newest tower construction in recent cycles and has drawn corporate innovation centres and tech hubs; Downtown has more older stock, a growing university presence and conversions to housing and hotels. Fintech and payments, logistics, film and television, media, healthcare and public health give the economy unusual breadth.

Retail and restaurant corridors

Peachtree Street in Midtown has steady weekday office and residential traffic, with restaurants and services at the base of towers. The Eastside Trail of the BeltLine has become the city's strongest food-and-drink corridor, with patios and food halls in converted buildings that draw crowds in the evenings and at weekends. Castleberry Hill suits galleries, bars and small venues near the stadium, and West Midtown mixes destination restaurants with design showrooms. Downtown retail is shaped by the event calendar: games, concerts and conventions bring surges, while ordinary evenings can be quiet, so operators there often plan around a published schedule of events. Buckhead's malls and streets cater to luxury and destination shopping. Across the city, visibility from main roads, convenient parking and outdoor seating carry more weight than in older, denser downtowns.

Coworking and flexible space

Flexible operators cluster in Midtown, especially around Tech Square and the Arts Center area, in Buckhead towers and in converted industrial buildings near the BeltLine and in West Midtown. Typical products are hot desks and dedicated desks on monthly memberships, private offices for small teams on terms from a month to a couple of years, and on-demand meeting rooms. Some university-linked incubators and accelerators offer space to start-ups in their programmes. Flexible space suits companies testing Atlanta, satellite teams of firms based elsewhere and growing start-ups that are not ready to commit to a long lease.

How commercial leases work here

Atlanta follows US conventions. Office rent is quoted per rentable square foot per year, and most in-town towers quote on a full-service gross basis, covering operating costs and property taxes for a base year, with your share of increases passed through afterwards. Some buildings use modified gross terms with separate electricity. Retail and many low-rise or creative buildings are triple net (NNN), where the tenant pays a share of property taxes, insurance and common-area maintenance on top of base rent. Annual rent escalations are standard, and parking is frequently charged separately from rent at a stated ratio of spaces per 1,000 square feet.

Landlords commonly offer a tenant improvement allowance and free-rent months scaled to the term and the tenant's credit, and many towers offer pre-built "spec" suites for smaller users. New and small businesses should expect to provide a personal guarantee, a larger security deposit or a letter of credit. Georgia does not charge sales tax on commercial rent. Businesses inside the city limits need a City of Atlanta business licence, renewed annually, and a certificate of occupancy for the space.

Getting your team there

MARTA's Red and Gold lines form the spine of in-town commuting, stopping at Five Points, Peachtree Center and Civic Center downtown, at North Avenue, Midtown and Arts Center in Midtown, and continuing north, with the Red Line serving Buckhead. Many Midtown and Buckhead towers sit on or next to stations, which is a real recruiting advantage. The airport is a direct MARTA ride from Downtown and Midtown, useful for teams that travel. Still, most of the region's workforce drives, so parking supply, cost and the route in from the interstates matter. The Downtown Connector is heavily congested at peak times, and game days bring closures around the stadium and arena. The BeltLine and protected bike lanes help staff who live in-town.

What to check before you sign

  • Parking: the ratio, monthly cost per space, reserved versus unreserved, visitor validation and electric-vehicle charging.
  • Walking distance to a MARTA station, and whether the route is pleasant in summer heat.
  • Event-day access: road closures, traffic and parking-deck pricing near the stadium, arena and convention centre.
  • After-hours cooling charges — essential in a long, hot summer — and backup power for storms.
  • Zoning: Downtown and Midtown fall under special public interest zoning districts, and BeltLine-adjacent sites have their own overlay rules on design, signage and use.
  • Licensing and occupancy: the City of Atlanta business licence and a certificate of occupancy for your use, especially for food, medical or assembly.
  • For creative and converted buildings, the condition of roofs, heating and cooling plant and floor loading.

Hosting events downtown

The Georgia World Congress Center, one of the largest convention centres in the country, sits beside Centennial Olympic Park with the stadium and arena next door, and the hotels around Peachtree Center offer large ballrooms. Museums such as the High Museum of Art and the National Center for Civil and Human Rights, the Atlanta Botanical Garden and the historic Fox Theatre suit receptions and gala dinners, while rooftops in Midtown and warehouses in West Midtown and Castleberry Hill work for launches and parties. Spring and autumn are peak seasons; summer heat and storms push events indoors, and major sports weekends and large conventions drive up hotel rates and fill venues early. Events in parks or on streets need permits from the city, and Piedmont Park bookings involve its conservancy, so plan ahead.

Is downtown the right base?

For most firms that want an urban address, Midtown is the strongest choice, with the newest buildings, MARTA access and the deepest talent pool. Downtown offers better value and suits government-facing, education, legal and event-related businesses, while Buckhead is the traditional home of finance and law. Suburban markets such as Perimeter Center, Cumberland and Alpharetta offer easier parking and proximity to where many employees live, but less walkable amenity. Weigh commute patterns and parking against the pull of an in-town address. For the wider picture of the city centre, see the downtown Atlanta guide.
