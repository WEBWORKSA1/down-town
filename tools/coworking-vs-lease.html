What the comparison includes

Coworking is priced as one monthly number per desk or per office, which bundles furniture, internet, cleaning, utilities and some meeting-room time. A lease splits the same things into rent, operating costs and one-off spending on fit-out, furniture and technology. The calculator puts both on a single timeline so you compare like with like.

The break-even is the month when the lease's lower running cost has paid back its upfront spending. If that month falls inside your horizon, the lease is cheaper overall; if not, coworking is — before you even put a value on flexibility.

What it leaves out

  • Flexibility. Being able to add or drop desks monthly has real value when headcount is uncertain.
  • Incentives. Rent-free months and fit-out allowances can transform a lease's economics; enter fit-out net of any allowance.
  • Exit costs. Reinstatement or dilapidations at the end of a lease, and the risk of paying for empty space if you shrink.
  • Deposits. Cash tied up in a deposit or bank guarantee is not a cost, but it matters to a young company.

The middle options

Between a hot desk and a ten-year lease sit fitted suites, managed offices (a provider runs a private floor for you) and subleases with furniture included. These often deliver most of a lease's cost advantage with a fraction of the commitment. Read coworking vs private office vs lease and subleasing office space.
