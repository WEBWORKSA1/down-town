What the comparison counts

The owning line adds your down payment, buying costs, mortgage payments, condo fees, property tax and maintenance, then subtracts the equity you would keep if you sold at the end — the home's value after selling costs, minus the remaining mortgage. The renting line adds rent, rising each year, plus the return your down payment and buying costs would have earned if invested instead.

Downtown-specific factors

  • Condo fees and reserves. Towers with pools, concierge and older mechanical systems carry higher fees, and underfunded reserves can mean special assessments. Read buying a downtown condo before you trust any fee figure.
  • Rental rules. Short-let and rental restrictions affect resale value and your flexibility if you move.
  • Supply. Downtowns with many new towers can see slower price growth; test a lower growth rate.

Using the result

Change one assumption at a time. If the answer flips with a small change in price growth or the number of years you stay, the decision is close and flexibility is worth more than the arithmetic. If owning wins comfortably across sensible assumptions, it is time to talk to a lender about pre-approval.
