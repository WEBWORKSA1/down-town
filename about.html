Why Down.Town exists

Downtowns are where a city concentrates its work, culture, transit, history and nightlife, and increasingly its homes. They are also changing faster than at any time in decades: office towers become apartments, roads become plazas, and storefronts find new uses.

Good information about them is surprisingly hard to find. Much of it is promotional, written by someone with a district to sell. The rest is scattered across a dozen official websites, each answering a different question. Down.Town puts it in one place, in the same structure for every city, written for the person who has a decision to make: where to spend a weekend, whether to move, where to open, how much space to lease.

We are independent. No city, tourism board, developer or downtown association owns, funds or approves what we publish.

How we research

Sources first, then the street

  1. Official sources

    City and transit agencies, tourism boards, official event organisers, universities, museums and published data. When two sources disagree, we go to the one closest to the fact.

  2. On-the-ground reporting

    Freelance city contributors who live in or know the downtowns we cover walk the districts, ride the lines and tell us what the official pages leave out.

  3. One structure for every city

    Every guide answers the same questions in the same order, so you can put Lisbon next to Calgary and compare them fairly.

  4. Annual review

    Each guide is reviewed at least once a year, and sooner when something important changes. The date of the latest review is shown on the page.

Nothing for sale in the guides

Our editorial guides describe districts, streets, public landmarks, markets, parks and transit. They do not recommend named restaurants, shops, brokers or landlords, so there is no spot in a guide for a business to buy its way into.

Where a business pays to appear on Down.Town, through a sponsorship or a Featured or Spotlight listing, the placement is labelled and kept apart from the editorial text.

The full rules are in our editorial policy, including how we score cities, how we handle corrections and how we use software tools.

Who we are

The Down.Town editors

Down.Town is made by a small, independent editorial team based in Montréal, working with freelance city contributors: writers, photographers, videographers and researchers who know the downtowns we cover.

Guides carry the byline "Down.Town editors" because each one is a team effort. A contributor brings local knowledge; an editor researches, checks the facts and edits the page before it is published. The editors decide what we cover, set the Index scores and are responsible for everything that appears on the site.

We may use software tools, including AI, to help research and draft; editors check facts and take responsibility for everything published.

Editorial coverage, a place in a guide, a recommendation or an Index score. Partners never pay for editorial coverage, and sponsors never review or approve it.

S The partner model, plainly

How free introductions work

  1. You tell us what you need

    A home, an office, a shopfront, a coworking desk, a venue or help relocating a team, through Get matched. It is free and commits you to nothing.

  2. An editor reads it

    A person reviews every request by hand, usually within one business day. Nothing is sent automatically.

  3. We introduce up to three partners

    Only if you ticked the consent box, and only partners who fit your city, size, budget and timing.

  4. You decide

    Partners contact you by your preferred method. Reply to whichever you like, or to none.

  5. A partner may pay us

    Depending on the arrangement, a partner pays Down.Town a fee for each introduction it accepts, a monthly fee as a city's featured partner, or a referral fee if you choose to work with it. You never pay us anything.

What partners never get

  • Your details without your consent
  • Lists of readers: we do not sell or rent personal information
  • Editorial coverage or a better Index score
  • Your newsletter sign-up, which is a separate, optional choice

Partners are independent

They are businesses in their own right, not Down.Town staff. Check licences, insurance and terms before you hire anyone. In most commercial markets the landlord pays a tenant broker's commission, so using one usually costs a tenant nothing extra. Read tenant rep brokers explained.

Your data, your call

Exactly what we share, with whom and for how long is set out in requests for introductions in our privacy policy. Are you a broker, agent, venue or mover? Join the partner network.

Independence

"Downtown" is an ordinary English word, and we use it in its ordinary sense. Down.Town is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by any city, government, business improvement district, downtown association, company or brand whose name includes the word. Details are in our trademark and copyright notice.

We pay our own way through the revenue listed above. Advertisers, sponsors and partners can buy clearly labelled space next to our coverage, never the coverage itself.

Corrections

Prices change, lines open, events move and we sometimes get things wrong. When we do, we fix it as quickly as we can, and note significant corrections on the page so readers can see what changed.

Seen something out of date? Send a correction with the page, what is wrong and, if you have one, a source. An editor reads every report.
