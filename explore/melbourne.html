In 30 seconds

  • Best for: café culture, food and live events
  • Trams are free within the CBD's Free Tram Zone
  • Weather changes quickly — locals joke about four seasons in one day
  • Big sport is a way of life; book ahead for the tennis, the races and the football finals

Melbourne's CBD is laid out on the Hoddle Grid, a rectangle of wide main streets cut through by narrow laneways. Those lanes — once service alleys — are now the city's signature: espresso bars, street art, rooftop bars and tiny restaurants tucked behind unmarked doors. The Yarra River marks the southern edge, with Southbank and its arts precinct on the far bank and Docklands spreading west.

The city takes its culture seriously, with galleries, libraries and theatres clustered around Federation Square and the river. Sport is a near-religion, and the major stadiums and tennis centre are an easy walk from the grid. What makes it work is the tram network and the density of small businesses at street level; what tests visitors is the weather, which can swing from hot to cold in an afternoon.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Federation Square. The city's public square, with the Australian Centre for the Moving Image.
  • Flinders Street Station. The famous yellow station building, a classic meeting point.
  • Hosier Lane. A laneway covered in constantly changing street art.
  • Queen Victoria Market. A historic market for produce, deli goods and night markets in season.
  • State Library Victoria. Known for its domed reading room, free to visit.
  • Royal Botanic Gardens. Large gardens a short walk south of the river.
  • National Gallery of Victoria. Australia's oldest public art gallery, split between international collections on St Kilda Road and Australian art at Federation Square; general entry is free.
  • Melbourne Cricket Ground. The city's sporting cathedral, a walk through the parks east of the CBD, with stadium tours and a sports museum on non-event days.

A perfect day downtown

Start with coffee in Degraves Street or Centre Place, then wander through the Block Arcade and the Royal Arcade. Walk up Swanston Street to State Library Victoria and look down on the domed reading room from the upper galleries, then cut back through the laneways for the street art of Hosier Lane and AC/DC Lane.

Graze your way through Queen Victoria Market for lunch (check its trading days first, as it closes on some weekdays), then ride a free tram back across the grid. Cross Princes Bridge to Southbank for the National Gallery of Victoria, and finish the afternoon with a walk in the Royal Botanic Gardens or along the Yarra.

For dinner, head to Chinatown or the Greek restaurants on Lonsdale Street, then up to a rooftop bar or into one of the theatres at the eastern end of the grid. In winter, an AFL match at the MCG is the most local night out you can have.

Eat and drink

Coffee is a local obsession, and the CBD is full of specialty cafés. The city's strong Greek, Italian, Vietnamese and Chinese communities shape its food, and the laneways hide some of its best small restaurants and bars.

Melbourne's coffee culture is built on independent roasters and the flat white, and a laneway espresso bar is the cheapest good experience in town. Little Bourke Street's Chinatown and the Greek precinct on Lonsdale Street are reliable for affordable meals, and the dumpling houses and noodle bars around the CBD's university campuses feed students well. Queen Victoria Market's deli and meat halls are perfect for picnic supplies, and its seasonal night markets add food stalls and music. A short tram ride takes you to Lygon Street's Italian restaurants in Carlton or the Vietnamese kitchens of Victoria Street in Richmond. Small-bar licences mean many of the best bars are hidden up stairs and down lanes, so look for signs on doors rather than shopfronts.

Getting around

The Free Tram Zone covers the CBD and Docklands. Metro trains run from Flinders Street and Southern Cross stations, with new underground stations improving CBD access. An express airport bus links Tullamarine with Southern Cross Station.

Melbourne's tram network is the largest in the world, and outside the free zone you touch on with myki. The underground City Loop links Flinders Street, Southern Cross, Flagstaff, Melbourne Central and Parliament stations, and the Metro Tunnel adds stations beneath Swanston Street at State Library and Town Hall. The flat grid makes walking and cycling easy, with protected bike lanes on several main streets. Drivers must master the hook turn, where right turns at many CBD intersections are made from the left lane, and parking is expensive. On Friday and Saturday nights, the Night Network keeps trains running through the night.

Where to stay

Stay inside the Hoddle Grid if you want to walk everywhere and use the free trams; streets near Flinders Street and Collins Street put the laneways on your doorstep. Southbank suits theatre-goers and anyone wanting river views, while Docklands is convenient for stadium events and apartment-style stays but quieter at night. East Melbourne, beside the Fitzroy and Treasury Gardens, is calm and close to the MCG. For neighbourhood character, Carlton and Fitzroy are a short tram ride north, and St Kilda makes a beach base to the south.

Living downtown

Apartment towers in the CBD, Southbank and Docklands house a large population, including many international students. Inner suburbs like Fitzroy, Carlton and Richmond offer older terrace houses within tram distance.

Residential leases in Victoria commonly run for 12 months. The bond, usually capped at one month's rent, is lodged with the Residential Tenancies Bond Authority rather than kept by the landlord, and rent can be increased only once every 12 months. Rental homes must meet minimum standards covering things such as heating, locks and mould. Inspect apartment towers carefully: build quality varies, and some smaller units were designed for the student market. Downtown suits students, young professionals and anyone who wants to walk to work and to events; families tend to choose the inner suburbs for houses and gardens.

Doing business downtown

Finance, education, professional services, hospitality and events drive the CBD. Café and small-bar businesses thrive on laneway foot traffic and a culture of eating out.

Collins Street is the traditional financial spine, with law firms and professional services concentrated in the western core and boutique offices toward the Paris end. Docklands holds several large corporate and bank headquarters on big modern floors, and St Kilda Road and Southbank add office stock south of the river. Tech and creative firms cluster in Cremorne and Richmond, and the University of Melbourne and RMIT anchor education and research on the CBD's northern edge. Retail is strongest on Bourke Street Mall, Swanston Street and in the arcades, with luxury brands toward the eastern end of Collins Street. Signature events

JanuaryAustralian Open — A tennis Grand Slam at Melbourne Park, walking distance from the CBD.
March–AprilMelbourne International Comedy Festival — A large comedy festival across the city.
Late SeptemberAFL Grand Final — The football season's finale at the MCG, preceded by a parade through the city.
Early NovemberMelbourne Cup — A famous horse race that brings the city to a stop.

Dates move from year to year. Check the organiser's official site before booking travel.

Seasons and weather

Summer (December to February) is warm and usually dry, but hot northerly winds can push temperatures very high before a sudden cool change sweeps through. Autumn (March to May) is mild, calm and golden, the most reliable season of the year. Winter (June to August) is cool, grey and damp rather than truly cold, with short days and frequent drizzle — and it is football season. Spring (September to November) is changeable, windy and showery. Pack layers whatever the month, a light waterproof jacket and sunscreen, as UV levels are high in summer even on mild days.

Safety and practicalities

The CBD is generally safe and stays busy late into the night, especially around Swanston, Bourke and Flinders Streets. Watch your bags in crowds at Flinders Street Station and during big events, and expect noisy streets around the King Street bars on weekend nights. Take care at tram stops in the middle of the road: cars must stop behind a tram when its doors open, but not every driver does. On extreme-heat days, drink water and stay in the shade, and in summer smoke from regional bushfires can occasionally affect air quality. Low-floor trams and level-access stops are expanding, but many older trams still have steps, so plan routes if you use a wheelchair. Mobile coverage is good across the centre, and tap water is safe to drink.

Local tips

  • Start your day with coffee in Degraves Street.
  • Use the free trams to hop across the grid.
  • Pack a layer — weather changes fast.
  • Check Queen Victoria Market's trading days before you go.
  • Book accommodation early around the Australian Open, the Melbourne Cup and the football finals.
  • If you drive in the CBD, learn the hook turn before you need it.
