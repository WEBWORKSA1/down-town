Downtown Melbourne

A street-grid CBD of laneway cafés, street art and free trams, built around coffee and culture.

Melbourne's CBD is laid out on the Hoddle Grid, a rectangle of wide main streets cut through by narrow laneways. Those lanes — once service alleys — are now the city's signature: espresso bars, street art, rooftop bars and tiny restaurants tucked behind unmarked doors.

The city takes its culture seriously, with galleries, libraries and theatres clustered around Federation Square and the river. Sport is a near-religion, and the major stadiums and tennis centre are an easy walk from the grid.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Federation Square. The city's public square, with the Australian Centre for the Moving Image.
  • Flinders Street Station. The famous yellow station building, a classic meeting point.
  • Hosier Lane. A laneway covered in constantly changing street art.
  • Queen Victoria Market. A historic market for produce, deli goods and night markets in season.
  • State Library Victoria. Known for its domed reading room, free to visit.
  • Royal Botanic Gardens. Large gardens a short walk south of the river.

Eat and drink

Coffee is a local obsession, and the CBD is full of specialty cafés. The city's strong Greek, Italian, Vietnamese and Chinese communities shape its food, and the laneways hide some of its best small restaurants and bars.

Getting around

The Free Tram Zone covers the CBD and Docklands. Metro trains run from Flinders Street and Southern Cross stations, with new underground stations improving CBD access. SkyBus links the airport with Southern Cross Station.

Living downtown

Apartment towers in the CBD, Southbank and Docklands house a large population, including many international students. Inner suburbs like Fitzroy, Carlton and Richmond offer older terrace houses within tram distance.

Questions people ask

What is the best area to stay in downtown Melbourne?

It depends on your plans. The laneways is the most central choice: Degraves Street, Centre Place and Hosier Lane — coffee, food and street art. For a different feel, consider Chinatown — Little Bourke Street, one of the oldest continuous Chinatowns in the Western world.

How do you get around downtown Melbourne?

The Free Tram Zone covers the CBD and Docklands. Walking is the best way to explore the core itself.

When is the best time to visit Melbourne?

Down.Town's pick is October–April. Signature events include Australian Open (January).

Is downtown Melbourne a good place to live?

Apartment towers in the CBD, Southbank and Docklands house a large population, including many international students. It scores 9/10 for walkability, 9/10 for transit and 4/10 for value in the Down.Town Index.
