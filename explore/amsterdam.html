Downtown Amsterdam

A UNESCO-listed canal ring where bicycles outnumber cars and the free ferries cross to the north.

Amsterdam's Centrum is defined by the 17th-century canal ring, a half-moon of concentric canals lined with narrow gabled houses. The ring is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and still works as a residential and commercial district.

Bikes rule the streets. Most locals cycle everywhere, and visitors who walk need to watch the bike lanes carefully. Free ferries behind Central Station carry people across the IJ to the creative district of Amsterdam-Noord.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Rijksmuseum. The national museum of Dutch art and history, home to Rembrandt's 'The Night Watch' and Vermeer's masterpieces.
  • Van Gogh Museum. The world's largest collection of Van Gogh's work, on Museumplein just south of the canal ring.
  • Anne Frank House. The canal-side house where Anne Frank and her family hid; tickets are sold online only and go quickly.
  • Dam Square and the Royal Palace. The city's central square, with the 17th-century former city hall turned royal palace.
  • Begijnhof. A hidden medieval courtyard of almshouses and gardens, a calm escape near the shopping streets.
  • Canal cruises. Boat trips through the UNESCO-listed canal ring — the most relaxed way to see the merchant houses.

Eat and drink

Herring stands, cheese shops, apple pie and Indonesian rijsttafel are the local classics, and 'brown cafés' — old wood-panelled pubs — are the place for a beer. Food halls in converted tram depots and the creative venues across the IJ in Noord have broadened the scene considerably.

Getting around

Trams run through the centre, the metro reaches the north and south, and trains from Central Station link to Schiphol in about 15–20 minutes. Free ferries cross the IJ. Cycling is the fastest way around.

Living downtown

Housing is very tight, and the centre is among the most expensive places to live in the Netherlands. The city limits holiday rentals with registration rules and a cap on nights per year, which has returned some homes to residents. Canal houses are beautiful but often have very steep stairs, and furniture is lifted in through the windows with hoisting beams.

Doing business downtown

Tourism, creative agencies, tech and European headquarters drive the economy, with the Zuidas district in the south as the main office hub. Central storefronts benefit from huge visitor numbers but operate under rules designed to protect residents and reduce overtourism, including limits on certain types of tourist-focused shops in the centre.

Local tips

  • Stay out of red bike lanes when walking — cyclists will not stop for you.
  • Take the free ferry behind Central Station to Amsterdam-Noord for views and creative venues.
  • Book the big museums ahead in spring and summer.

Questions people ask

What is the best area to stay in downtown Amsterdam?

It depends on your plans. Canal Ring is the most central choice: the canal belt of grand merchant houses. For a different feel, consider Jordaan — a neighbourhood of small canals, cafés and boutiques.

How do you get around downtown Amsterdam?

Trams run through the centre, the metro reaches the north and south, and trains from Central Station link to Schiphol in about 15–20 minutes. Walking is the best way to explore the core itself.

When is the best time to visit Amsterdam?

Down.Town's pick is April–September. Signature events include King's Day (April 27).

Is downtown Amsterdam a good place to live?

Housing is very tight, and the centre is among the most expensive places to live in the Netherlands. It scores 10/10 for walkability, 9/10 for transit and 3/10 for value in the Down.Town Index.
