Downtown Sydney

A harbour downtown of ferries, sandstone laneways and an opera house, newly threaded with light rail and metro.

Sydney's Central Business District runs south from Circular Quay, where ferries, the Opera House and the Harbour Bridge frame one of the most recognisable waterfronts in the world, toward Central Station and the old markets of Haymarket.

The CBD has changed noticeably over the past decade. Light rail now runs along a largely pedestrianised George Street, a metro line cuts beneath the city with new stations, and the former container wharves at Barangaroo have become a new district of offices, restaurants and a headland park.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Sydney Opera House. The sail-roofed performing arts centre, with guided tours and harbourside bars.
  • Sydney Harbour Bridge. Walk the free pedestrian path or book a guided climb over the arch.
  • Royal Botanic Garden. Harbourside gardens leading to Mrs Macquarie's Chair viewpoint.
  • Queen Victoria Building. A grand Victorian shopping arcade on George Street.
  • Art Gallery of New South Wales. A major gallery whose newer building opened in 2022.
  • Barangaroo Reserve. A headland park recreating the harbour's pre-colonial shoreline.

Eat and drink

Sydney is known for café breakfasts, seafood and strong Asian cooking, especially Thai, Chinese and Vietnamese. Haymarket's Chinatown and the laneways around Angel Place are good for casual meals; Barangaroo and Circular Quay offer harbour-view dining.

Getting around

Sydney Trains' City Circle loops under the CBD, the metro adds fast underground stations, and light rail runs along George Street. Ferries from Circular Quay reach Manly, Taronga Zoo and many harbour suburbs. The airport rail line includes a station access fee.

Living downtown

Apartment towers in Pyrmont, Ultimo, Haymarket and Barangaroo put residents within walking distance of work. Sydney is one of the most expensive housing markets in Australia, so many people trade space for a short commute.

Doing business downtown

Finance and professional services dominate Martin Place and Barangaroo, with tourism and retail across the CBD. Busy laneways and the light-rail corridor have created new opportunities for small food and retail businesses.

Local tips

  • Walk from the Opera House through the Botanic Garden to Mrs Macquarie's Chair.
  • Wear sun protection; UV levels are high even on cloudy days.
  • Check ferry timetables for the last services back from the beaches.

Questions people ask

What is the best area to stay in downtown Sydney?

It depends on your plans. Circular Quay and The Rocks is the most central choice: the ferry hub and the sandstone colonial district beside the bridge. For a different feel, consider CBD core — Martin Place, Pitt Street Mall and the office towers in between.

How do you get around downtown Sydney?

Sydney Trains' City Circle loops under the CBD, the metro adds fast underground stations, and light rail runs along George Street. Walking is the best way to explore the core itself.

When is the best time to visit Sydney?

Down.Town's pick is September–November, March–May. Signature events include Vivid Sydney (Late May–June).

Is downtown Sydney a good place to live?

Apartment towers in Pyrmont, Ultimo, Haymarket and Barangaroo put residents within walking distance of work. It scores 8/10 for walkability, 8/10 for transit and 3/10 for value in the Down.Town Index.
