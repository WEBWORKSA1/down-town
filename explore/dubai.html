Downtown Dubai

The world's tallest tower, a financial free zone and old trading creeks — a desert city with two centres.

Dubai's modern centre is the master-planned district around the Burj Khalifa, the tallest building in the world, along with the neighbouring financial free zone and Business Bay. It is a district of malls, fountains and towers built in barely two decades. (The developer markets this district under its own brand name; Down.Town is not connected to it.)

The older centre lies along Dubai Creek, in Deira and Bur Dubai, where wooden boats still carry passengers across the water and souks sell gold, spices and textiles. Seeing both halves is the best way to understand the city.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Burj Khalifa. The world's tallest building, with observation decks.
  • Dubai Fountain. Choreographed fountain shows in the evening.
  • Dubai Opera. A performing-arts venue near the fountain lake.
  • Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood. Restored wind-tower houses and museums in Bur Dubai.
  • Dubai Creek abras. Traditional wooden boats crossing the creek for a small fare.
  • Gold and Spice Souks. Traditional markets in Deira.

Eat and drink

Dubai's dining ranges from hotel restaurants run by internationally known chefs to small Indian, Pakistani, Iranian, Lebanese and Emirati eateries in the older districts, where a full meal costs a fraction of the tower-top price. Brunches on Fridays and weekends are a social institution. The financial free zone has many upscale restaurants and bars.

Getting around

Dubai Metro's Red Line runs along Sheikh Zayed Road, serving the Burj Khalifa district, the free zones and the airport; the Green Line covers Old Dubai. A Nol card pays for metro, tram and buses. Taxis and ride-hailing are common and inexpensive by global standards. Walking between districts is impractical in summer heat.

Living downtown

Most residents rent apartments in towers, with annual leases often paid in one to four cheques. The UAE does not levy personal income tax, which draws many professionals. Summer heat shapes daily life: most activity moves indoors from June to September, and air-conditioning costs should be included in your budget.

Doing business downtown

Trade, finance, real estate, tourism and logistics dominate. The financial free zone operates its own common-law courts, and dozens of free zones offer company-setup routes with full foreign ownership. A federal corporate tax applies to profits above a threshold. Retail in mall-based districts is highly competitive; street-level independents do better in older neighbourhoods.

Questions people ask

What is the best area to stay in downtown Dubai?

It depends on your plans. Burj Khalifa district is the most central choice: towers, the fountain lake, a giant mall and an opera house. For a different feel, consider DIFC — the financial free zone, with galleries and restaurants.

How do you get around downtown Dubai?

Dubai Metro's Red Line runs along Sheikh Zayed Road, serving the Burj Khalifa district, the free zones and the airport; the Green Line covers Old Dubai. Walking is the best way to explore the core itself.

When is the best time to visit Dubai?

Down.Town's pick is November–March. Signature events include Dubai Shopping Festival (Dec–Jan).

Is downtown Dubai a good place to live?

Most residents rent apartments in towers, with annual leases often paid in one to four cheques. It scores 5/10 for walkability, 7/10 for transit and 4/10 for value in the Down.Town Index.
