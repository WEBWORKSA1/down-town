Downtown Boston

Colonial history and a modern harbourfront in a downtown small enough to cross on foot in 20 minutes.

In 30 seconds

  • Best for: American history, universities and a walkable harbourfront
  • Follow the Freedom Trail's red line between historic sites
  • Downtown is small; walking is often faster than transit for short hops

Boston's downtown is compact, crooked and historic. Streets that began as cow paths and colonial lanes twist between the Financial District's towers, Downtown Crossing's shopping streets and the old market buildings around Faneuil Hall.

The city's biggest modern change was burying the elevated highway that once split downtown from the waterfront. In its place, the Rose Kennedy Greenway runs as a chain of parks, fountains and food trucks, and the harbour edge is walkable again.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Freedom Trail. A 2.5-mile line of red brick connecting 16 historic sites from Boston Common to Charlestown.
  • Boston Common and Public Garden. America's oldest public park and the formal garden next to it, with seasonal Swan Boats.
  • Faneuil Hall Marketplace. Historic market halls including Quincy Market, busy with food stalls and street performers.
  • Old State House. A colonial government building surrounded by modern towers.
  • Rose Kennedy Greenway. A linear park built over the buried highway, with art installations and seasonal markets.
  • New England Aquarium. A large aquarium on the harbour, with whale-watch boats leaving nearby.

Eat and drink

Seafood leads: oysters, clam chowder and lobster rolls. The North End's Italian bakeries and trattorias sit minutes from the Financial District, and the Seaport has added a cluster of larger restaurants and bars along the water.

Getting around

The MBTA's subway lines meet downtown at Park Street, Downtown Crossing, State and Government Center. The Silver Line bus rapid transit runs to Logan Airport, and commuter rail leaves from South Station and North Station. Bluebikes and water taxis fill the gaps.

Living downtown

Downtown housing includes historic row houses in Beacon Hill, apartments in the North End and new towers in the Seaport. Boston has a large student population, so leases often start September 1, and competition peaks in summer.

Doing business downtown

Finance, biotech, healthcare, higher education and consulting drive the region. Downtown Crossing has seen investment in new residential and retail space. Many businesses tie their calendar to the academic year and the visitor season.

Local tips

  • Walk the Freedom Trail in the morning, then end in the North End for lunch.
  • Take a harbour ferry for a cheap view of the skyline.
  • Avoid moving on September 1 if you can — it's the city's busiest moving day.

Questions people ask

What is the best area to stay in downtown Boston?

It depends on your plans. Downtown Crossing is the most central choice: a pedestrian shopping district at the heart of downtown. For a different feel, consider Financial District — office towers between the Greenway and Post Office Square.

How do you get around downtown Boston?

The MBTA's subway lines meet downtown at Park Street, Downtown Crossing, State and Government Center. Walking is the best way to explore the core itself.

When is the best time to visit Boston?

Down.Town's pick is May–June, September–October. Signature events include Boston Marathon (April).

Is downtown Boston a good place to live?

Downtown housing includes historic row houses in Beacon Hill, apartments in the North End and new towers in the Seaport. It scores 10/10 for walkability, 8/10 for transit and 3/10 for value in the Down.Town Index.
