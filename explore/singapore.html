Downtown Singapore

A garden city-state where the financial district meets the bay, hawker centres and colonial avenues.

Singapore's Downtown Core covers the Central Business District around Raffles Place, the newer towers of Marina Bay built on reclaimed land, and the colonial Civic District along the Singapore River. Chinatown's shophouses and temples sit just to the south-west.

It is one of the most deliberately planned downtowns in the world: covered walkways for tropical downpours, generous parks, strict upkeep and a transit network that brings most of the island within an hour. The payoff is a business district that also works as an evening and weekend destination.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Gardens by the Bay. Huge conservatories and the Supertree Grove, with a free light-and-sound show most evenings.
  • Marina Bay Sands SkyPark. An observation deck across the top of three hotel towers.
  • Merlion Park. The city's half-lion, half-fish symbol facing the bay.
  • National Gallery Singapore. Southeast Asian art inside the former Supreme Court and City Hall.
  • Lau Pa Sat. A Victorian cast-iron market hall whose side street becomes a satay strip at night.
  • Buddha Tooth Relic Temple. A large Tang-style temple and museum in Chinatown.

Eat and drink

Hawker centres are Singapore's great equaliser: office workers, families and food critics queue at the same stalls for chicken rice, laksa, char kway teow and satay. Within the CBD, Lau Pa Sat and the Maxwell and Amoy Street centres are lunchtime institutions. Tanjong Pagar and Keong Saik Road hold many of the city's most interesting restaurants and bars.

Getting around

The MRT network covers the island, and several lines intersect beneath the CBD and Marina Bay. Changi Airport is on the East–West Line with a change at Tanah Merah. Contactless bank cards work across trains and buses, and taxis and ride-hailing are widely used. Covered walkways link many towers to stations.

Living downtown

Most foreign professionals rent private condominiums, which come with pools and security; public housing flats are largely for citizens and permanent residents. Rents near the CBD and Marina Bay are among Asia's highest, so many residents choose areas a few MRT stops away. Leases are commonly one or two years.

Doing business downtown

Singapore is a global financial centre and a popular location for Asia-Pacific headquarters, with English as the business language, a competitive corporate tax regime and efficient company registration. Retail and food businesses face high rents but a dense, high-spending daytime population.

Local tips

  • Carry an umbrella — tropical showers arrive fast.
  • Order at hawker stalls by tapping a card or paying cash; tables are shared.
  • Respect strict local rules on littering, smoking areas and eating on public transport.

Questions people ask

What is the best area to stay in downtown Singapore?

It depends on your plans. Raffles Place is the most central choice: the financial core of bank towers beside the river mouth. For a different feel, consider Marina Bay — waterfront promenades, the integrated resort, gardens and new office towers.

How do you get around downtown Singapore?

The MRT network covers the island, and several lines intersect beneath the CBD and Marina Bay. Walking is the best way to explore the core itself.

When is the best time to visit Singapore?

Down.Town's pick is February–April (drier). Signature events include Chinese New Year (Jan–Feb).

Is downtown Singapore a good place to live?

Most foreign professionals rent private condominiums, which come with pools and security; public housing flats are largely for citizens and permanent residents. It scores 8/10 for walkability, 10/10 for transit and 3/10 for value in the Down.Town Index.
