In 30 seconds

  • Best for: architecture, museums and lakefront parks within a 20-minute walk
  • Take an architecture boat tour on the river — it's the fastest way to read the skyline
  • Winters are cold and windy; the Pedway tunnels help in January

The Loop takes its name from the elevated train tracks that circle its core, and the rattle of the 'L' overhead is still the sound of downtown Chicago. Inside that ring sits one of the world's great collections of commercial architecture, from early steel-frame skyscrapers to glass towers along the river. Beyond it, downtown spreads north across the river to River North and the Magnificent Mile, west to the restaurant-packed West Loop, and south to the museums and lofts of the South Loop.

What makes the Loop work for visitors and residents is the green edge: Grant Park and Millennium Park run along Lake Michigan, and the Chicago Riverwalk turns the river's south bank into a promenade of cafés, kayak launches and boat docks. Few downtowns put this much open space this close to the office towers. Add one of the best transit networks in North America and a food scene that runs from hot-dog stands to tasting menus, and you have a downtown that rewards days of exploring, as long as you respect the winter.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Millennium Park. Home to the mirrored 'Cloud Gate' sculpture, the Crown Fountain and free summer concerts at the Pritzker Pavilion.
  • Art Institute of Chicago. One of the most important art museums in North America, right on Michigan Avenue.
  • Chicago Riverwalk. A multi-level promenade along the main branch of the river, with seasonal bars and boat landings.
  • Willis Tower Skydeck. Glass ledges hanging off the 103rd floor of one of the city's tallest towers.
  • Chicago Cultural Center. A free landmark building with two stained-glass domes and rotating exhibitions.
  • Buckingham Fountain and Grant Park. A grand lakefront fountain with seasonal water shows in the park that hosts the city's biggest festivals.
  • Museum Campus. The Field Museum, Shedd Aquarium and Adler Planetarium share a lakefront peninsula south of Grant Park, with some of the best skyline views in the city.
  • Navy Pier. A long public pier on Lake Michigan with a Ferris wheel, a Shakespeare theatre, lake cruises and summer fireworks.

A perfect day downtown

Start with a morning architecture cruise on the river, which explains the skyline better than any book. Walk off the boat along the Riverwalk, then cut through the Loop under the 'L' tracks to admire the early skyscrapers on LaSalle and Dearborn streets and the stained-glass domes of the Chicago Cultural Center.

Spend the afternoon in Millennium Park and the Art Institute of Chicago, which needs at least three hours even if you focus on a few galleries. On a fine day, walk south through Grant Park past Buckingham Fountain to the Museum Campus for the classic skyline view from the lakefront path.

In the evening, take the Green or Pink Line to the West Loop for dinner along Randolph Street and Fulton Market, then catch a show in the Loop theatre district or a set at a jazz or blues club. In summer, end with a drink on the Riverwalk as the towers light up.

Eat and drink

Chicago's downtown food scene splits between quick lunch spots for office crowds and destination dining just outside the Loop in Fulton Market and River North. Try a deep-dish pizza once, an Italian beef sandwich, and a tasting menu if the budget allows. Locals eat tavern-style pizza, a thin, cracker-crisp pie cut into squares, far more often than deep dish, and the Chicago-style hot dog comes "dragged through the garden" with mustard, onions, relish, tomato, pickle and sport peppers, never ketchup.

The Riverwalk's seasonal terraces are the best place for an after-work drink on a warm evening. For variety and value, ride the Red Line to Chinatown, the Pink Line to Pilsen for Mexican bakeries and taquerías, or walk to Greektown on Halsted Street in the West Loop. Food halls inside Loop office buildings and train stations make fast, decent weekday lunches, and many high-end restaurants offer cheaper lunch or bar menus.

Getting around

CTA trains converge on the Loop: the Blue Line runs to O'Hare and the Orange Line to Midway. The Brown, Green, Orange, Pink and Purple lines circle the elevated Loop itself, while the Red and Blue lines run in subways beneath it and operate around the clock. Metra commuter trains terminate at Union Station, Ogilvie and Millennium stations, as well as LaSalle Street Station, and the South Shore Line runs from Millennium Station to north-west Indiana. Union Station is also the Midwest's main Amtrak hub. Pay with a Ventra card, the Ventra app or a contactless bank card or phone.

The Pedway, a network of underground and enclosed walkways, links many buildings, and Divvy bikes cover the lakefront trail, which runs for miles along the shore. Walking is the best way to explore the core, and the grid, with its numbered address system, is easy to read. Driving is rarely worth it downtown: garages are expensive, Lower Wacker Drive's multi-level roads confuse first-timers, and DuSable Lake Shore Drive jams at rush hour.

Where to stay

Stay in the Loop for theatres, Millennium Park and the easiest access to every train line; it is quieter at night than the neighbourhoods around it. River North puts you among galleries, restaurants and nightlife, while the Magnificent Mile and Streeterville suit shoppers and families heading to Navy Pier and the lakefront. The West Loop is the food lover's base, and the South Loop works for the Museum Campus and conventions at McCormick Place. Prices spike during major conventions and festival weekends, so check the calendar before booking.

Living downtown

Residential towers line Lakeshore East and the South Loop, and converted lofts fill Printer's Row. Compared with coastal cities, Chicago gives more space for the rent. Expect to budget for heating in winter and to plan around lake-effect weather. Streeterville and River North add high-rise rentals with doormen and amenity floors, the West Loop mixes new towers with warehouse lofts, and older courtyard buildings and two-flats are a short train ride away in neighbourhoods such as Lincoln Park and Logan Square.

Most people rent on 12-month leases after a credit check and application. Chicago's Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance sets strict rules on security deposits, so many landlords charge a non-refundable move-in fee instead; read the lease carefully either way. Rental agents are commonly paid by the landlord. Illinois has a flat state income tax, and Cook County property taxes are built into rents and condo assessments. Downtown suits professionals, students, retirees who want to give up the car, and anyone who values transit and culture over a yard.

Doing business downtown

Finance (including major derivatives exchanges), law, consulting and corporate headquarters anchor the Loop. State Street is the traditional retail spine, while Fulton Market attracts tech offices and restaurant groups. Convention traffic at McCormick Place brings waves of business visitors. Newer trophy towers line the river along Wacker Drive, while older buildings on LaSalle Street are being repositioned, some as apartments under a city conversion initiative.

River North draws advertising, design and media firms, and Streeterville is dominated by hospitals and university medical and law schools. Retail splits between State Street's department stores and flagships, the Magnificent Mile's big-brand and luxury stores, and the restaurant row along Randolph Street in the West Loop. Seasons and weather

Summer is Chicago's reward: warm to hot, sometimes humid, with beaches, festivals and patios at full strength, and a lake breeze that keeps the shoreline cooler than the inland neighbourhoods. Autumn is crisp and often clear, ideal for walking. Winter is long and cold, with biting wind off the lake and occasional heavy snow; pack a proper insulated coat, a hat, gloves and waterproof boots from December to March. Spring arrives late and unpredictably, with chilly, grey days near the lake even in May, so bring layers.

Safety and practicalities

Downtown is busy and well policed in the main visitor areas, but the usual big-city rules apply: watch your phone on crowded trains and platforms, where snatch thefts happen, and keep bags closed on Michigan Avenue and at festivals. The Loop is quieter late at night and on weekend evenings than River North or the West Loop, so plan your route home. On late-night trains, ride in the first car near the operator. Many 'L' stations downtown have lifts, but not all, so check accessibility before you travel. In winter, frostbite is a genuine risk on the coldest days; cover exposed skin and use the Pedway. Mobile coverage is strong, though it drops in parts of the Pedway and on Lower Wacker.

Local tips

  • Board an architecture river cruise early in your trip for orientation.
  • Check Millennium Park's summer calendar for free concerts and films.
  • Use the Pedway in winter to cross the Loop without facing the wind.
  • Tap a contactless bank card on CTA turnstiles instead of buying a separate card for a short stay.
  • Walk or cycle the lakefront trail early on a summer morning for empty paths and skyline views.
