Business trips, layovers and weekend breaks often leave you with a single free day in a city you don't know. Guidebooks list fifty things to do; you have time for five. We use the same simple framework for every downtown we cover. It works in Chicago and Singapore, Montréal and Melbourne.

The method rests on three ideas. Get the big picture early, so everything after makes sense. See one thing properly rather than many things badly. And let the light set the timetable: views in the clear morning, shade and food at midday, water and skyline as the day ends.

The day, slot by slot

Morning: orient from above or on the water

Start with the big picture. Go up something tall — an observation deck, a hill, a rooftop — or take a boat. From above, you'll see how the downtown is laid out: where the river or harbour is, where the historic core sits, where the towers cluster, which way the streets run. Many cities have free or cheap options: Tokyo's Metropolitan Government Building, London's Sky Garden, the Staten Island Ferry in New York, the Star Ferry in Hong Kong.

Go early. Queues are shorter, the light is clearer, and you'll spend the rest of the day knowing where you are.

Late morning: walk the historic spine

Every downtown has a spine — the oldest street, the main square, the route between the original port and the cathedral or city hall. Walk it end to end. You'll pass the most important buildings, see how the city grew and get a feel for its character. In Boston it's the Freedom Trail; in Mexico City it's Calle Madero to the Zócalo; in Barcelona it's the Gothic Quarter's lanes.

Pick one museum or landmark on that route and go inside. One museum done properly beats four done in a rush.

Lunch: eat where locals work

At midday, follow office workers. Food halls, markets and hawker centres are fast, affordable and show what the city actually eats. Singapore's hawker centres, Melbourne's laneway cafés, Toronto's St. Lawrence Market and Seattle's Pike Place Market are all within a short walk of the business district.

Afternoon: pick one neighbourhood and slow down

After lunch, choose a single neighbourhood next to the core and explore it slowly. Look for a district with a distinct identity: a former warehouse area now full of studios, a historic Chinatown, an arts district, a waterfront. Wander side streets, step into independent shops and stop at a café. This is where you'll find the city's personality rather than its postcard.

Late afternoon: green space or water

Most great downtowns have a green or blue edge: a lakefront park, a riverwalk, a harbour promenade, a botanical garden. Spend an hour there as the day cools. It's where residents go after work, so you'll see the city at its most relaxed.

Dusk: find a view

End the day with the skyline lighting up. A bridge, a ferry, a waterfront or a rooftop bar works. If you went up high in the morning, try a view from the water or across a river at night. You'll see the downtown you walked through all day from the outside.

Dinner: one booking, then wander

If you want a special meal, book one place in advance. Otherwise, head back to the neighbourhood you liked most in the afternoon and let yourself wander. Many downtowns are livelier at night than during the day.

Make it a habit

  • Morning: view from above or on the water.
  • Late morning: walk the historic spine; one landmark inside.
  • Lunch: market or food hall.
  • Afternoon: one neighbourhood, slowly.
  • Late afternoon: park, river or harbour.
  • Dusk: skyline view.
  • Evening: one great meal or a wander.

Adapting the method

On a layover

With six hours or less in the city, do the view and the spine and nothing else. Check first that your passport and visa allow you to leave the airport, and that a fast rail link exists: Hong Kong's Airport Express, Singapore's MRT from Changi, and London's Elizabeth line or Heathrow Express all run into the centre. Allow generous time to get back through security.

In rain, cold or heat

Swap the outdoor slots for covered ones. In winter, Montréal's underground city (RÉSO) and Toronto's PATH network link offices, stations and shops below the streets. In the heat of Singapore or Dubai, move the walking to dawn and dusk and spend the middle of the day in museums, markets and covered arcades.

On a Sunday or holiday

Check opening days before you plan. In Germany, most shops close on Sundays; in Paris, the Louvre closes on Tuesdays and the Musée d'Orsay on Mondays. Business districts such as the City of London can be eerily quiet at weekends, which is bad for lunch but excellent for photographs of empty streets.

With children

Shorten the walking legs, put a playground or park after lunch, and count ferries and trams as rides rather than transport. Choose a museum with hands-on galleries, and build in a rest at your hotel in the early afternoon.

On a work trip

If your only free time is an evening and an early morning, split the method: dusk and dinner on the first day, then the view and the spine before your first meeting.

Pacing, transport and money

Plan the walking in loops of two or three kilometres between sit-down stops, and use transit for the one long hop of the day — usually from the historic core to your afternoon neighbourhood. In many cities you can now tap a contactless bank card or phone at the gate instead of buying a ticket; London caps daily contactless fares, and Singapore and Sydney accept contactless cards on most public transport. Elsewhere, a day pass is usually the simplest option.

The view and the museum are the day's biggest costs. If you're watching the budget, our guide to free things to do downtown has a free alternative for almost every slot.

Practical tips

  • Use contactless payment on transit in cities that support it — no ticket machines to figure out.
  • Download an offline map before you go.
  • Wear comfortable shoes; you'll walk 15–20 km on a good day.
  • Leave luggage at your hotel or a station locker.
  • Check opening days — many museums close one day a week.
  • Look up the sunset time the night before, and plan your dusk viewpoint around it.
  • Carry a refillable water bottle and a light layer for the evening.
  • Leave one hour unplanned. It's usually the best part of the day.
