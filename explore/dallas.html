In 30 seconds

  • Best for: big-name architecture and museums, Texas history and a serious business trip with a good night out attached
  • Ride the free M-Line trolley between the Arts District and Uptown
  • Spring storms and summer heat are the two weather facts to plan around

Downtown Dallas is the cluster of towers inside a tight ring of freeways on the east bank of the Trinity River. The historic Main Street District holds the middle, the West End and Dealey Plaza sit on the river side, the Arts District runs along the northern edge, and Deep Ellum begins just across the freeway to the east. Uptown, the city's densest neighbourhood, spills north over a park built on a freeway deck.

Dallas built its downtown for business, and it shows in the parking garages and the quiet blocks at weekends. But the city has spent heavily on culture and public space: a concentrated arts district with buildings by architects including I.M. Pei, Renzo Piano and Norman Foster, Klyde Warren Park over the Woodall Rodgers Freeway, and a light-rail network that meets in the core. Combine the downtown grid with Deep Ellum and Uptown and you get a far more lively centre than the old office-park image suggests.

The districts

Main Street DistrictEarly skyscrapers, grand bank and department-store buildings, Main Street Garden Park and many of downtown's converted loft residences.
Arts DistrictMuseums, concert halls and theatres along Flora Street on the northern edge of downtown, with Klyde Warren Park next door.
West End and Dealey PlazaRed-brick warehouses, the Sixth Floor Museum and the Old Red Museum in the 1890s county courthouse.
Deep EllumEast of the freeway, a historic blues and jazz district now packed with murals, live-music rooms, bars and creative offices.
Farmers Market and the CedarsThe Dallas Farmers Market's sheds and food hall anchor a growing residential pocket in the south-east; the Cedars, south of I-30, adds studios and lofts.
Uptown and Victory ParkNorth of the freeway: dense apartments, restaurants along McKinney Avenue, the start of the Katy Trail and the arena shared by the Mavericks and the Stars.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Klyde Warren Park. A deck park built over a sunken freeway, with food trucks, a children's area, lawn games and free events that stitch downtown to Uptown.
  • Dallas Museum of Art. A large encyclopaedic collection, from ancient Mediterranean art to contemporary work, with free general admission.
  • Nasher Sculpture Center. A Renzo Piano–designed gallery and garden of modern and contemporary sculpture, across the street from the museum.
  • The Sixth Floor Museum at Dealey Plaza. A sober, detailed account of the Kennedy assassination inside the former book depository. Timed tickets sell out at busy times.
  • Reunion Tower. The illuminated ball-topped tower on the western edge of downtown, with an observation deck looking over the skyline and the Trinity River.
  • Pioneer Plaza. A herd of bronze longhorn steers and cowboys commemorating the cattle drives, beside a historic cemetery near City Hall.
  • Perot Museum of Nature and Science. A hands-on science museum in a striking cube-shaped building on the edge of Victory Park; a strong choice with children.
  • Fair Park. A collection of Art Deco exhibition halls from the 1936 Texas Centennial, home of the State Fair and the Cotton Bowl, a short DART ride east.

A perfect day downtown

Begin at the Sixth Floor Museum when it opens, then walk across Dealey Plaza to the John F. Kennedy Memorial Plaza and the Old Red Museum, which covers Dallas County's history from its frontier days. Continue past Pioneer Plaza's bronze herd and through the Main Street District for a look at the city's early towers.

Head north to the Arts District for the afternoon. Spend a couple of hours in the Dallas Museum of Art, cross the street to the Nasher's garden, and eat lunch from the food trucks at Klyde Warren Park. With children, swap the Nasher for the Perot Museum. Then ride the free M-Line trolley up McKinney Avenue for a wander through Uptown.

Watch the sunset from Reunion Tower or from the Margaret Hunt Hill Bridge, Santiago Calatrava's white-arched span over the Trinity. Have dinner in Uptown or Deep Ellum, then catch a concert at the Meyerson Symphony Center or a band in one of Deep Ellum's music rooms.

Eat and drink

Dallas eats big and it eats Texan: Tex-Mex enchiladas and fajitas, smoked brisket and sausage, chicken-fried steak and, every autumn, an inventive parade of deep-fried creations at the State Fair. Mexican taquerías in Oak Cliff, Korean restaurants in the northern suburbs and a long list of Vietnamese, Indian and Ethiopian kitchens reflect how international the metro area has become. Uptown and the Arts District lean polished, while Deep Ellum mixes pizza, tacos and late-night bar food.

For a cheap, good meal, use the food trucks at Klyde Warren Park, the food hall at the Dallas Farmers Market and weekday lunch specials in the Main Street District. Take the streetcar over the river to the Bishop Arts District for independent cafés and bakeries. Weekday happy hours are a local institution; downtown bars fill straight after work and thin out early.

Getting around

Dallas Area Rapid Transit, or DART, runs the light-rail network, and all of its Red, Blue, Green and Orange lines pass through downtown on the transit mall along Pacific Avenue and Bryan Street. The Orange Line runs to DFW Airport, and the Trinity Railway Express commuter train links Union Station with Fort Worth, stopping near the airport on the way. For Love Field, ride the Green Line to the Inwood/Love Field station and take the connecting shuttle bus. The free M-Line vintage trolley runs through Uptown to the Arts District, and the Dallas Streetcar crosses the Trinity from Union Station to the Bishop Arts District. Pay with the GoPass app, a GoPass Tap card or a contactless bank card.

The downtown grid is walkable, and a network of tunnels and skybridges links some office towers, but the freeway loop and empty blocks can make walks between districts feel longer than they are. The Katy Trail, a former rail line, gives cyclists and runners a green route north from Victory Park. Most Dallas residents drive: garages are plentiful, meters are paid by app, and traffic on the freeway loop is heavy at rush hour.

Where to stay

The Main Street District is the most central base for business travel and puts you on the light-rail mall. The Arts District and the edge of Uptown suit culture lovers who want to walk to museums, Klyde Warren Park and McKinney Avenue's restaurants. Stay near Reunion and Union Station for sightseeing and train connections, and in Deep Ellum if your trip is about music and nightlife. If you have a very early flight, hotels at or near the airports are easier than a dawn drive across the metroplex. The convention centre is in the middle of a multi-year rebuild, so check which halls your event is using before you book.

Living downtown

Many downtown Dallas homes are in converted office and bank buildings along Main Street and Elm Street, alongside newer towers in the Arts District and Victory Park. Deep Ellum, the Farmers Market area and the Cedars add mid-rise blocks and townhouses, while Uptown, just north, is the city's densest rental neighbourhood. Leases usually run 12 months, with an application fee, credit and background checks, an income requirement and a security deposit; pets often bring an extra deposit and monthly pet rent, and parking may be charged separately.

Apartment locators are widely used in Texas and are normally paid by the landlord; see our guide to apartment locators and rental agents. Texas has no state personal income tax, but property taxes are relatively high and are built into rents. Dallas's electricity market is deregulated, so you choose a retail electricity provider when you sign up for power. Downtown suits young professionals, people working in the core or Uptown, and anyone who wants light rail on the doorstep; families often prefer the leafier neighbourhoods further out.

Doing business downtown

Dallas–Fort Worth is one of the largest metropolitan economies in the United States and a magnet for corporate headquarters relocations. Downtown and Uptown hold banks, insurers, law firms, telecom and energy companies, and the Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas sits on the edge of the Arts District. A fast-growing financial-services cluster, nicknamed "Y'all Street", has added asset managers and trading operations to the mix.

The central business district competes with Uptown and Victory Park, where newer towers command a premium, and with suburban office hubs in Las Colinas, Plano and Frisco. Retail in the core leans on ground-floor restaurants and services along Main Street and Elm Street, with independent shops, bars and venues in Deep Ellum and boutiques and dining in Uptown. Signature events

Late September–OctoberState Fair of Texas — Big Tex, livestock shows, a midway and famously creative fried food fill Fair Park's Art Deco grounds, a short DART ride east.
OctoberRed River Rivalry — The University of Texas and the University of Oklahoma play their annual football game at the Cotton Bowl during the fair.
DecemberDallas Marathon — The marathon, half marathon and relay start and finish downtown near City Hall.

Dates move from year to year. Check the organiser's official site before booking travel.

Seasons and weather

Summers are long and hot, with highs often passing 100°F in July and August; the heat is drier than on the Gulf Coast but the sun is relentless, so carry water and plan outdoor time for mornings and evenings. Spring is beautiful but volatile: Dallas sits near the southern end of the Plains storm belt, and thunderstorms can bring large hail and tornado warnings, so take alerts seriously. Autumn is the most comfortable season. Winters are mostly mild, but occasional ice storms can close roads and slow the city to a halt. Pack sun protection in summer, a rain jacket in spring and warm layers from December to February.

Safety and practicalities

The busiest areas are generally comfortable, but some downtown blocks are quiet after office hours, so walk on active streets at night and use ride-hail for longer late trips. Deep Ellum gets crowded at weekends; stay with your group around closing time. Car break-ins are the most common problem, so leave nothing on view. DART trains run less often late at night, so check your last connection. Light rail offers level boarding and most of the Arts District is step-free. Severe-weather alerts come through your phone; if a tornado warning sounds, move to an interior room on a low floor.

Local tips

  • General admission to the Dallas Museum of Art is free, so you can visit in short bursts.
  • Book timed tickets for the Sixth Floor Museum ahead of weekends and holidays.
  • Take the DART Green Line to Fair Park during the State Fair rather than driving.
  • Walk or cycle the Katy Trail early in the morning before the heat.
  • If you move here, compare retail electricity plans carefully; contract terms vary widely.
