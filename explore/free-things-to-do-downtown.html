Free Things to Do in Any Downtown

Observation decks, museums, ferries, markets, festivals and walks — the categories of free experiences that almost every city centre offers.

By the Down.Town editorsUpdated October 20262 min read

Downtowns have a reputation for being expensive, and hotel and restaurant prices often prove it. Yet most city centres offer a surprising number of free experiences. Here are the categories to look for in any downtown, with examples from cities we cover.

Free views

Paid observation decks are popular, but many cities have free alternatives. Tokyo's Metropolitan Government Building has free observation decks. London's Sky Garden is free with an advance booking. Montréal's Mount Royal lookout is a walk from downtown. Bridges, ferries and hilltop parks offer views without a ticket.

Free museums and galleries

Some cities have long traditions of free museums. In Washington, D.C., most Smithsonian museums are free. In London, many national museums, including Tate Modern, are free to enter for their permanent collections. Many other museums offer free days, free evening hours or free entry to certain galleries. Check museum websites for current schedules.

Free boat rides

Commuter ferries can be the best-value harbour cruises. New York's Staten Island Ferry is free and passes the Statue of Liberty. Amsterdam's ferries across the IJ to the north of the city are free. Elsewhere, ferries are often cheap enough to feel free.

Free transit

Some downtowns run free transit: Melbourne's Free Tram Zone covers the CBD, and Miami's Metromover is free. These double as sightseeing routes.

Markets

Browsing markets costs nothing. Pike Place in Seattle, St. Lawrence in Toronto, Queen Victoria in Melbourne and Borough in London are attractions in their own right.

Parks, waterfronts and walks

Riverwalks, seawalls, botanical gardens and historic walking trails are free and often the most memorable part of a visit. Chicago's Riverwalk and Millennium Park, Vancouver's seawall and Boston's Freedom Trail are examples.

Festivals and events

Many downtown festivals include free outdoor events: Montréal's jazz festival stages, Chicago's Blues Festival, Toronto's Nuit Blanche and Paris's Fête de la Musique. Our festival calendar lists signature events by city.

Architecture and public art

Lobbies of historic buildings, cathedrals, public art and street art are free. Melbourne's Hosier Lane and Miami's Wynwood are well-known street-art destinations.

Libraries

Central libraries are often architectural landmarks with free exhibitions and quiet spaces: Seattle Central Library and State Library Victoria in Melbourne are two examples.

A free-day plan

Combine a free view in the morning, a free museum, a market lunch you pay for, a free park or waterfront walk, and a free festival event or street art in the evening. You'll see more of the city than many paid tours show.
