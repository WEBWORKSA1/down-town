In 30 seconds

  • Best for: live music from late morning to late night, country-music history and a walkable, high-energy core
  • Visit Lower Broadway on a weekday afternoon for the music without the weekend crush
  • Book hotels well ahead for CMA Fest, the Fourth of July and big football weekends

Downtown Nashville climbs from the west bank of the Cumberland River up to the Tennessee State Capitol on its hill. Lower Broadway, the neon strip of honky-tonks, runs down to the riverfront; SoBro, south of Broadway, holds the convention centre, the symphony hall and the Country Music Hall of Fame; and the Gulch, a former rail yard, fills the south-west corner with apartments and offices. Across the river, the East Bank is being rebuilt around a new stadium.

Few American downtowns have changed as quickly. Cranes, new towers and a steady flow of visitors have turned a once-quiet government and banking centre into one of the country's busiest nightlife districts, and on weekends the bachelor and bachelorette parties can be hard to miss. The trade-off is real: transit is thin and prices have climbed. But the music is genuinely everywhere, the history is easy to reach on foot, and the neighbourhoods just outside downtown are some of the most enjoyable in the South.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Ryman Auditorium. The 1892 former tabernacle known as the "Mother Church of Country Music" and home of the Grand Ole Opry from 1943 to 1974; daytime tours, evening concerts and superb acoustics.
  • Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum. Costumes, instruments, recordings and the Hall of Fame rotunda; it also runs tours to Historic RCA Studio B on Music Row.
  • Tennessee State Capitol. A Greek Revival capitol completed in 1859 on the city's highest hill, with free tours and the tomb of President James K. Polk in its grounds.
  • Bicentennial Capitol Mall State Park. A long state park below the Capitol that tells Tennessee's story in granite, fountains and gardens.
  • Tennessee State Museum. A free museum of state history, from early Native American cultures to the Civil War and civil rights, at the edge of the mall.
  • Frist Art Museum. Rotating exhibitions in the Art Deco former main post office beside the old Union Station.
  • National Museum of African American Music. Interactive galleries on Broadway tracing Black musical traditions from spirituals and blues to gospel, jazz and hip-hop.
  • John Seigenthaler Pedestrian Bridge. A restored truss bridge over the Cumberland with the best skyline view in town, linking SoBro and the East Bank.

A perfect day downtown

Start on Capitol Hill: tour the State Capitol, walk down the Bicentennial Mall and spend an hour in the free Tennessee State Museum. The Nashville Farmers' Market next door is a good place for an early lunch, with a food hall and seasonal produce stalls.

In the afternoon, head to SoBro for the Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum. Allow at least two or three hours, and add the Studio B tour if you care about the recording history of the city. Walk through the Frist or the National Museum of African American Music if time allows, then cross the pedestrian bridge at golden hour for the skyline.

For the evening, eat hot chicken or a plate of Southern sides, then catch a show at the Ryman or a songwriter round in a listening room. Finish with a walk down Lower Broadway, ducking into whichever honky-tonk has the band you like best, and end the night in Printers Alley.

Eat and drink

Nashville's signature dish is hot chicken: fried chicken coated in a cayenne-heavy paste, served on white bread with pickles, at heat levels that run from mild to genuinely painful. The meat-and-three, a Southern cafeteria-style lunch of one meat and three sides, is the other local institution, alongside biscuits, barbecue and Tennessee whiskey. Nolensville Pike, south of downtown, is lined with Kurdish, Mexican, Middle Eastern and East African kitchens that reflect the city's immigrant communities.

Lower Broadway's bars are built for tourists and priced accordingly, so eat elsewhere and come back for the music. The Farmers' Market food hall, meat-and-three lunches and food trucks are the best value downtown. The Gulch, Germantown and East Nashville have the most interesting restaurants, and midweek reservations are far easier to get than weekend ones.

Getting around

WeGo Public Transit runs Nashville's buses, and most routes meet at WeGo Central downtown. You can tap a contactless bank card or phone to pay, and a WeGo bus route links the airport with downtown. The WeGo Star commuter train runs from Riverfront Station east to Lebanon, mainly on weekdays. Transit coverage is limited by big-city standards, which is why the Index score is low, though voters approved a long-term plan in 2024 to improve bus service, sidewalks and traffic signals across the county.

Downtown itself is compact, but it is hilly: the walk from the riverfront up to the Capitol is a steady climb. Nashville BCycle stations and the Cumberland River Greenway serve cyclists, and e-scooters are common. Pedal taverns and tractor-pulled party wagons share the streets around Broadway, so take care when crossing. Most visitors arrive by car or ride-hail; garages are plentiful but expensive on event nights, and the interstates that meet around downtown are congested at rush hour.

Where to stay

SoBro and the blocks around Lower Broadway are the choice for first-timers who want to walk everywhere, but rooms facing Broadway can be loud until the early hours. The Gulch is slightly calmer and more upscale, with good restaurants on the doorstep. Germantown and the streets near the Capitol suit couples and food-focused travellers, while Midtown and West End, near Vanderbilt University and Music Row, offer more bars and often better value. East Nashville has character and short-term rentals but needs a car or ride-hail. Book well ahead for CMA Fest, the Fourth of July, New Year's Eve and home football weekends.

Living downtown

Most downtown homes are in new high-rise rental and condominium towers in SoBro, the Gulch and North Gulch, with a smaller stock of lofts in the historic buildings along Second Avenue and Printers Alley. Germantown and Salemtown, just north, add townhouses and restored brick homes. Leases usually run 12 months, with an application fee, credit and background checks and a security deposit; new buildings sometimes offer a free month or reduced rent to fill units, and parking is often extra.

Tennessee has no state income tax on wages, but sales tax is relatively high. Electricity comes from Nashville Electric Service, the public utility, so there is no supplier to choose. Downtown suits professionals in healthcare, music and government, sports fans and people who want nightlife on the doorstep. If you need quiet or a yard, the neighbourhoods a few miles out are a better fit; our guide to downtown versus suburban costs can help you weigh it up.

Doing business downtown

Nashville is a national centre for the healthcare-management industry, which runs hospital groups and health services companies from offices across the city. Music and entertainment are the city's calling card, with labels, publishers and rights organisations concentrated on Music Row, and state government, finance, insurance, tourism and the universities round out the economy. Corporate operations hubs and technology employers have added offices downtown and on the East Bank in recent years.

The central business district's towers along Church and Commerce streets compete with newer space in SoBro and the Gulch, with Midtown and Music Row, and with suburban hubs in Brentwood and Cool Springs. Retail is dominated by tourism: Lower Broadway's bars, boot stores and souvenir shops trade from late morning to late night, while the Gulch has boutiques and the Fifth Avenue galleries host a monthly art crawl. Signature events

Late MarchTin Pan South Songwriters Festival — Songwriters play the hits they wrote in intimate rounds at venues across the city.
JuneCMA Fest — Country music's big fan festival, with stadium concerts at night and free daytime stages around downtown and the riverfront.
JulyLet Freedom Sing! Fourth of July — A free riverfront concert followed by one of the country's larger fireworks shows over the Cumberland.
WinterOpry at the Ryman — The Grand Ole Opry returns to its former home for a run of winter shows.

Dates move from year to year. Check the organiser's official site before booking travel.

Seasons and weather

Spring is lovely and lively, with blossoms and patio weather, but it is also storm season: severe thunderstorms and the occasional tornado warning are part of life in Middle Tennessee, so keep alerts switched on. Summer is hot and humid from June to September, with afternoon storms; pack light clothes and comfortable shoes for the hills. Autumn is the sweet spot, warm by day and crisp by night. Winters are cool and grey, with occasional snow or ice that can disrupt roads for a day or two. Bring a rain jacket in any season and a warm layer for winter evenings on Broadway.

Safety and practicalities

Downtown is busy and generally safe in the main entertainment areas, but crowds bring pickpockets, so keep phones and wallets secure on Broadway. Keep an eye on your drink and your group late at night, and agree a meeting point in case phones die. Ride-hail pick-up points near Broadway get chaotic at closing time; walking a few blocks away first usually helps. Watch for party vehicles and scooters when crossing. Many honky-tonks are multi-level with steep stairs, though larger venues, including the Ryman, offer accessible seating. Take storm and heat warnings seriously, and drink water between drinks in summer. Mobile coverage is strong, though it can slow in big crowds.

Local tips

  • Tip the band; song requests usually come with a tip in the jar.
  • Book Ryman shows and tours early, especially in winter when the Opry is in residence.
  • Cross the pedestrian bridge at sunset for the classic skyline photo.
  • Plan the Grand Ole Opry as a separate outing; its main home is several miles east of downtown.
  • Eat on Nolensville Pike or in East Nashville at least once to escape the Broadway prices.
