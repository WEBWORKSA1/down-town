In 30 seconds

  • Best for: garden-city parks, a famous pub and coffee culture, and India's technology economy
  • Ride Namma Metro to beat the traffic; plan road trips across town with plenty of time to spare
  • The climate is mild all year thanks to the city's elevation; October to February is the sweet spot

Bengaluru has no single downtown. Its historic centre is the Central Business District, the grid of avenues around MG Road, Brigade Road and Residency Road that grew up beside the colonial-era Cantonment, together with the green expanse of Cubbon Park and the granite bulk of Vidhana Soudha, the state legislature. To the west lie the older bazaar districts of the original town, the pete, around Chickpet and the City Market.

What makes Bengaluru distinctive is that most of its modern economy grew outside this core. The technology parks that made the city famous line the Outer Ring Road, Whitefield in the east, Electronic City in the south and the corridor towards the airport in the north. The CBD remains the place for government, older companies, shopping, parks and nightlife, while the corridors hold the campuses. Traffic between them is notoriously slow, so the metro, which now links the centre with several of these hubs, is your best friend.

The districts

MG Road and Brigade RoadThe commercial spine, with the elevated metro above MG Road, shopping on Brigade Road and bookshops, cafés and pubs on Church Street.
Cubbon Park and Vidhana SoudhaThe civic quarter: the legislature, the red High Court building, the State Central Library and museums set in a large, shady park.
Vittal Mallya Road and Lavelle RoadThe upmarket pocket of the CBD, with a luxury mall-and-office complex, smart restaurants and some of the city's best-known bars.
Commercial Street and ShivajinagarCrowded lanes of clothing, fabric and jewellery shops, the historic Russell Market and St Mary's Basilica.
Chickpet and the old peteThe original market town: wholesale silk and saree shops, the City Market and the remains of the old fort.
Indiranagar and UlsoorA short metro ride east: Ulsoor Lake, an old temple quarter and the cafés, boutiques and pubs of 100 Feet Road.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Vidhana Soudha. The vast granite seat of the Karnataka legislature, completed in the 1950s in a neo-Dravidian style; admire it from outside, as access is restricted.
  • Cubbon Park. The green lungs of the CBD, laid out in the 1870s, with bamboo groves, the State Central Library and early-morning joggers.
  • Attara Kacheri. The striking red Greco-Roman building facing Vidhana Soudha, now the Karnataka High Court.
  • Government Museum and Venkatappa Art Gallery. Neighbouring museums on the edge of Cubbon Park covering archaeology, sculpture and Karnataka's painters.
  • Lalbagh Botanical Garden. An eighteenth-century garden begun under Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan, with a Victorian glasshouse and ancient rock outcrops; reached by the Green Line.
  • Tipu Sultan's Summer Palace. A carved teak palace near the City Market, a reminder of the city's pre-colonial past.
  • Bangalore Palace. A turreted, Tudor-style royal residence north of the CBD, set in grounds that also host big concerts and exhibitions.
  • National Gallery of Modern Art. Modern and contemporary Indian art in a restored mansion on Palace Road.

A perfect day downtown

Start early, as locals do, with idli, vada and strong filter coffee at a darshini, one of the city's fast, standing-room tiffin halls. Walk off breakfast in Cubbon Park, then circle past the High Court and Vidhana Soudha before the heat builds. The Government Museum, Venkatappa Art Gallery and the National Gallery of Modern Art are all close by.

In the afternoon, take the metro to the old city. Explore the lanes of Chickpet, the flower and produce stalls of the City Market and Tipu Sultan's Summer Palace, then continue south to Lalbagh for a late-afternoon stroll under its old trees. If you prefer shopping, Commercial Street and Brigade Road are the classic choices.

For the evening, head to Church Street or Lavelle Road for a craft beer or a cocktail; Bengaluru helped start India's brewpub boom. Alternatively, ride the Purple Line to Indiranagar, where 100 Feet Road and the lanes around it are lined with restaurants and bars.

Eat and drink

Bengaluru is one of India's great breakfast cities. Darshinis serve masala dosa, idli, vada, kesari bath and filter coffee at speed and for very little money, and many open early. Lunch can be a full south Indian meal on a banana leaf, an Andhra-style meal with fiery pickles and gunpowder, or the local favourite, ragi mudde (finger-millet balls) with a spicy curry. Coastal Mangalorean seafood, Chettinad cooking and the city's distinctive biryani served in leaf cups are all worth seeking out, and "military hotels", old-school non-vegetarian eateries, are a local institution.

Street food centres on V.V. Puram's food street in the south of the city in the evening, and on Shivajinagar during Ramadan, when the lanes fill with kebab and sweet stalls. The CBD and Indiranagar hold the city's famous pub scene, alongside specialty-coffee roasters drawing on the plantations of the Western Ghats. To eat well cheaply, follow the crowds: busy stalls with high turnover are the safest bet. Drink bottled or filtered water.

Getting around

Namma Metro, operated by BMRCL, is the fastest way through the city. The Purple Line runs east to west from Whitefield through MG Road, Cubbon Park and Vidhana Soudha to the south-west; the Green Line runs north to south through Majestic, Chickpet, the City Market and Lalbagh; and the newer Yellow Line serves the southern tech belt towards Electronic City, with further lines, including one to the airport, under construction. City buses run by BMTC are cheap and extensive but slow in traffic. From Kempegowda International Airport, airport buses and app-booked cabs run into the city; the journey can take well over an hour at peak times.

Walking in the CBD has improved with redesigned streets around Church Street, St Mark's Road and Cubbon Park, but pavements elsewhere can be broken or blocked, and crossing busy roads takes care. Autorickshaws are everywhere; insist on the meter or book through an app. Cycling is possible in parks and early in the morning but not much fun in traffic. Driving and parking in the centre are stressful, and congestion on the Outer Ring Road and at major junctions is legendary, so plan cross-city meetings around the metro and outside peak hours.

Where to stay

For a short leisure trip, stay around MG Road, Residency Road, Richmond Road or St Mark's Road, within walking distance of the park, the museums and the main shopping and pub streets. Lavelle Road and Vittal Mallya Road are the upmarket option. Indiranagar suits younger travellers who want cafés and nightlife on a metro line. Business visitors should match their base to their meetings: Whitefield, the Outer Ring Road and Electronic City each have hotels and serviced apartments that can save hours of daily driving. For an early flight, consider the north of the city towards the airport.

Living downtown

Central Bengaluru offers a mix of older bungalows, low-rise apartment buildings and newer towers, with leafy neighbourhoods such as Richmond Town, Langford Town and the streets off Cunningham Road commanding a premium. Most newer housing, especially gated apartment complexes with pools, gyms and power backup, has been built near the tech corridors. Residential rentals in India commonly use an 11-month leave-and-licence or rental agreement, printed on stamp paper, and Bengaluru landlords are known for asking higher security deposits than many Indian cities. Negotiate the deposit, document the flat's condition and agree in writing what can be deducted at the end, such as repainting.

Living centrally suits people who work in the CBD or nearby districts, who want to walk to parks and restaurants, and who value metro access. Professionals whose offices are on the Outer Ring Road or in Whitefield usually live near work instead, because daily cross-city commutes by car can be punishing. Families weigh school locations heavily, and many of the best-known schools are spread across the city rather than concentrated in the centre.

Doing business downtown

The CBD is home to the state government, the High Court, banks, law firms, consulates and older corporate offices along MG Road, Residency Road, Richmond Road, Infantry Road and Cunningham Road. The city's technology economy is spread across the corridors: the Outer Ring Road, especially from Marathahalli to Sarjapur Road and around Hebbal, holds dense clusters of tech parks, Whitefield has some of the largest IT campuses, Electronic City is one of India's earliest electronics hubs, and the north is growing fast towards the airport. Koramangala and HSR Layout are the heart of the startup scene. Global capability centres of multinational companies, aerospace, biotechnology and research institutions such as the Indian Institute of Science add depth.

Retail in the centre is concentrated on Brigade Road, Commercial Street and MG Road, with luxury brands clustered around Vittal Mallya Road and wholesale trade in Chickpet and the old pete. For districts, lease structures and what to check, see Office and commercial space in central Bengaluru.

Dates move from year to year. Check the organiser's official site before booking travel.

Seasons and weather

At roughly 900 metres above sea level, Bengaluru is milder than India's coastal and northern cities. October to February is the most pleasant period: sunny days, cool mornings and evenings, and occasional late-season showers in October and November; bring a light layer for the evenings. March to May is the warm season, with hot afternoons and dramatic thunderstorms towards its end. The southwest monsoon, from June to September, brings frequent rain rather than the deluges of Mumbai, but low-lying roads flood quickly and traffic slows to a crawl. Pack an umbrella, breathable clothing and shoes that cope with puddles.

Safety and practicalities

Bengaluru is generally considered one of the more relaxed big Indian cities for visitors. Watch for pickpockets in crowded markets, at the Majestic bus and rail hub and on packed buses, and keep your phone secure in autorickshaws. Traffic is the biggest everyday hazard: cross carefully, and watch for uneven pavements and open drains. The metro closes around midnight on most lines, so book a cab for late returns and share your trip details. Mosquitoes increase during the monsoon, so carry repellent. Accessibility is improving in metro stations and newer buildings but is limited on older streets. Mobile data is fast and cheap, and UPI payments work almost everywhere.

Local tips

  • Group meetings by corridor; crossing the city twice in a day can eat an afternoon.
  • Breakfast is the best value meal of the day; arrive early before darshinis get busy.
  • Ask autorickshaw drivers to use the meter, or book through an app to avoid haggling.
  • Remove shoes and dress modestly when visiting temples and some churches.
  • A few words of Kannada, such as namaskara (hello) and dhanyavadagalu (thank you), go a long way.
  • Carry a light rain jacket from June to November.
