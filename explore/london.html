Downtown London

Two downtowns in one: a medieval square mile of finance and a West End of theatres, galleries and shops.

London doesn't have a single downtown. The City of London — the 'Square Mile' — is the historic financial core, with its own local government, Roman wall fragments and lanes of medieval width squeezed between glass towers. Two miles west, the West End is the city's entertainment and shopping centre.

Between them run the Thames, the South Bank's cultural institutions and one of the densest transit networks on earth. A good day here means walking from St Paul's across the Millennium Bridge to Tate Modern, then following the river toward Westminster.

Landmarks worth your time

  • St Paul's Cathedral. Christopher Wren's domed masterpiece with climbable galleries.
  • Tower of London and Tower Bridge. A royal fortress that holds the Crown Jewels, beside the famous bascule bridge.
  • Sky Garden. A free public garden at the top of a City tower — book a slot in advance.
  • Leadenhall Market. An ornate Victorian covered market among the office towers.
  • Borough Market. A historic food market just across London Bridge.
  • Tate Modern. A free modern art museum in a converted power station.

Eat and drink

Borough Market and Leadenhall Market are the obvious starts. Soho and Covent Garden pack in everything from Chinatown's dim sum to tasting menus, and Shoreditch is strong on casual and late-night food. Pubs remain the best value for a sit-down meal in the City.

Getting around

The Underground's Bank and Liverpool Street stations are major City interchanges, and the Elizabeth line links Heathrow, the West End, the City and Canary Wharf. Buses, Santander Cycles hire bikes and river boats complete the network.

Living downtown

Few people live in the City itself, though the Barbican Estate is a landmark exception. Clerkenwell, Shoreditch and the South Bank fringe offer flats with short commutes. Rents are among Europe's highest.

Doing business downtown

The City is a global centre for banking, insurance and law, with Canary Wharf as a second financial hub. The West End is retail and hospitality's flagship district, with high rents and very high footfall.

Questions people ask

What is the best area to stay in downtown London?

It depends on your plans. The City of London is the most central choice: the Square Mile of banks, insurers, Wren churches and new towers. For a different feel, consider West End — Soho, Covent Garden and the theatre district.

How do you get around downtown London?

The Underground's Bank and Liverpool Street stations are major City interchanges, and the Elizabeth line links Heathrow, the West End, the City and Canary Wharf. Walking is the best way to explore the core itself.

When is the best time to visit London?

Down.Town's pick is May–September. Signature events include London Marathon (April).

Is downtown London a good place to live?

Few people live in the City itself, though the Barbican Estate is a landmark exception. It scores 10/10 for walkability, 10/10 for transit and 2/10 for value in the Down.Town Index.
