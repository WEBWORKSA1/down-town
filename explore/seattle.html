Downtown Seattle

A market, a waterfront park and ferry rides to the islands, set between big-tech campuses and the Sound.

Downtown Seattle climbs steeply from Elliott Bay toward First Hill, which is why so many of its best views come from street ends and stairways. Pike Place Market anchors the centre, and a rebuilt waterfront has replaced the elevated highway that once cut the city off from the water.

Big tech changed the city's north edge: South Lake Union and the Denny Triangle filled with offices and apartment towers. The older districts — Pioneer Square's brick buildings and the International District — give downtown its historic texture.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Pike Place Market. A working market of fishmongers, farmers and craftspeople since 1907.
  • Seattle Central Library. A striking glass-and-steel library designed by the Office for Metropolitan Architecture.
  • Seattle Great Wheel. A Ferris wheel on Pier 57 over Elliott Bay.
  • Seattle Art Museum. The city's main art museum, a short walk from the market.
  • Space Needle. Reached by the 1962 monorail from Westlake Center to Seattle Center.
  • Olympic Sculpture Park. A free, outdoor sculpture park stepping down to the waterfront.

Eat and drink

Seattle's food identity is seafood and coffee. Pike Place Market is the place for oysters, chowder and fresh salmon; the International District serves Chinese, Vietnamese and Japanese food. Belltown and Capitol Hill hold the evening dining scene.

Getting around

Link light rail connects Sea-Tac Airport with downtown and continues north and east. The monorail runs to Seattle Center, streetcars serve South Lake Union and First Hill, and Washington State Ferries leave Colman Dock for Bainbridge Island and Bremerton. King Street Station handles Amtrak and Sounder trains.

Living downtown

Downtown housing is mostly new apartment and condo towers in Belltown, the Denny Triangle and South Lake Union, with older buildings around First Hill. Washington has no state personal income tax, though sales and property taxes are relatively high.

Doing business downtown

Cloud computing and e-commerce headquarters dominate the north end, with aerospace, biotech and the port in the wider region. Pike Place supports a large community of small vendors, and the waterfront redevelopment is creating new ground-floor retail and food space.

Local tips

  • Visit Pike Place early on a weekday before the crowds.
  • Take the Bainbridge Island ferry for a cheap skyline cruise.
  • Walk the waterfront's new Overlook Walk connecting the market to the shore.

Questions people ask

What is the best area to stay in downtown Seattle?

It depends on your plans. Pike Place and Belltown is the most central choice: the market and its surrounding blocks of restaurants, bars and apartments. For a different feel, consider Waterfront — a new park promenade, piers, the aquarium and the Great Wheel.

How do you get around downtown Seattle?

Link light rail connects Sea-Tac Airport with downtown and continues north and east. Walking is the best way to explore the core itself.

When is the best time to visit Seattle?

Down.Town's pick is June–September. Signature events include Seattle International Film Festival (May–June).

Is downtown Seattle a good place to live?

Downtown housing is mostly new apartment and condo towers in Belltown, the Denny Triangle and South Lake Union, with older buildings around First Hill. It scores 9/10 for walkability, 8/10 for transit and 4/10 for value in the Down.Town Index.
