Downtown New York

The original skyscraper downtown, rebuilt around parks, ferries and a 24-hour mix of offices and homes.

In 30 seconds

  • Best for: first-time visitors who want icons, harbour views and history in one walk
  • Base yourself near Fulton Street or the World Trade Center for the widest choice of subway lines
  • Weekends are quieter on the business blocks and busier on the waterfront

Lower Manhattan is where New York began, and it still behaves like the city's front door. The tip of the island packs the Financial District, Battery Park City, the Seaport and Tribeca into a walkable wedge bordered by water on three sides, with ferries, subway lines and the PATH all converging within a few blocks.

The district has changed character more than once in the past two decades. Office towers have been converted into apartments, waterfront promenades have replaced parking and service roads, and the streets that used to empty out at 6 p.m. now hold residents walking dogs, school runs and restaurant crowds well into the evening.

The districts

Financial DistrictNarrow colonial-era street plan under glass and limestone towers; Wall Street, Stone Street and the Stock Exchange façade.
Battery Park CityA planned waterfront neighbourhood of parks, playgrounds and esplanades on the Hudson — the family-friendly side of downtown.
SeaportRestored 19th-century counting houses, cobblestones and piers on the East River, with views of the Brooklyn Bridge.
TribecaFormer warehouse blocks turned into lofts, galleries and well-known restaurants just north of the financial core.

Landmarks worth your time

  • 9/11 Memorial & Museum. Twin reflecting pools set in the footprints of the original towers; the museum below tells the story of the day and the recovery.
  • One World Trade Center. The tallest building in the Western Hemisphere, with an observatory on its upper floors.
  • Battery Park and the harbour ferries. Departure point for boats to the Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island, and a green edge for a picnic.
  • Brooklyn Bridge. Walk the elevated promenade from City Hall Park for the classic skyline view back toward downtown.
  • Stone Street. A short cobbled lane of historic buildings that fills with outdoor tables in warm weather.
  • Staten Island Ferry. A free commuter ferry from Whitehall Terminal that passes the Statue of Liberty both ways.

Eat and drink

Downtown eating ranges from food halls inside office complexes to old taverns and some of the city's most-booked dining rooms in Tribeca. Stone Street and the Seaport handle groups and after-work crowds; for quick bites, the food halls around Brookfield Place and Fulton Street keep lines short. Book ahead for Friday and Saturday nights in Tribeca.

Getting around

More subway lines meet here than almost anywhere in the city, with Fulton Center and the World Trade Center hub as the main interchanges. The PATH runs under the Hudson to New Jersey, NYC Ferry routes leave from Pier 11 and Battery Park City, and the free Staten Island Ferry runs around the clock. Citi Bike docks are dense, and the Hudson River Greenway gives cyclists a protected route north.

Living downtown

Housing is mostly rental towers, a growing number of converted office buildings, and condos in Battery Park City and Tribeca. Expect high rents, doormen buildings with amenities, and small kitchens. Families gravitate to Battery Park City for its parks and schools; younger renters favour the Financial District for value per square foot relative to Midtown.

Doing business downtown

Finance, insurance, law and media remain anchors, joined by tech and creative firms that like the transit access. Street-level retail clusters around Fulton Street, the Oculus and Brookfield Place, and the Seaport. Foot traffic peaks on weekday lunch hours and tourist weekends, so many independent shops plan two different weekly rhythms.

Local tips

  • Visit the Memorial early in the morning for a quieter experience.
  • Ride the Staten Island Ferry at sunset for free harbour views.
  • Use the Oculus and Fulton Center underground passages on rainy days.

Questions people ask

What is the best area to stay in downtown New York?

It depends on your plans. Financial District is the most central choice: narrow colonial-era street plan under glass and limestone towers; Wall Street, Stone Street and the Stock Exchange façade. For a different feel, consider Battery Park City — a planned waterfront neighbourhood of parks, playgrounds and esplanades on the Hudson — the family-friendly side of downtown.

How do you get around downtown New York?

More subway lines meet here than almost anywhere in the city, with Fulton Center and the World Trade Center hub as the main interchanges. Walking is the best way to explore the core itself.

When is the best time to visit New York?

Down.Town's pick is April–June, September–November. Signature events include Tribeca Festival (June).

Is downtown New York a good place to live?

Housing is mostly rental towers, a growing number of converted office buildings, and condos in Battery Park City and Tribeca. It scores 10/10 for walkability, 10/10 for transit and 2/10 for value in the Down.Town Index.
