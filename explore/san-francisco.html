Downtown San Francisco

Cable cars, a ferry-building food hall and a rooftop park — a compact downtown reinventing itself after remote work.

Financial District, Union Square & SoMa, United StatesDown.Town Index 76/100Reviewed October 2026

Downtown San Francisco packs the Financial District, Union Square's shopping blocks, Chinatown and the South of Market district into a few hilly square kilometres between Market Street and the bay. It is one of the most transit-rich centres in the United States.

The pandemic hit downtown offices hard, and the city has spent the years since repurposing space: pop-up shops in vacant storefronts, new events on Market Street and homes in former offices. For visitors, the classics remain — cable cars, the Ferry Building and views from every hilltop.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Ferry Building Marketplace. A restored ferry terminal now home to food producers and a well-known farmers' market.
  • Cable cars. The world's last manually operated cable car system, running from Powell and Market and California Street.
  • Chinatown. One of the oldest Chinatowns in North America, entered through the Dragon Gate on Grant Avenue.
  • Salesforce Park. A long rooftop park built on top of the downtown transit centre.
  • SFMOMA. The San Francisco Museum of Modern Art, with large free-to-enter ground-floor galleries.
  • Coit Tower. An Art Deco tower on Telegraph Hill with murals inside and views of the bay.

Eat and drink

San Francisco built much of modern American food culture around local produce, and the Ferry Building is its showroom. Chinatown and nearby North Beach offer dim sum and Italian cafés within walking distance of the Financial District. SoMa hosts many of the city's ambitious restaurants.

Getting around

BART runs under Market Street to SFO and across the bay to Oakland and Berkeley. Muni Metro, buses, historic streetcars on the F-line and the cable cars cover the city. Ferries leave the Ferry Building for Sausalito, Oakland and other bay towns. Bay Wheels bike share is widely available.

Living downtown

Residential options include SoMa lofts, high-rise apartments in Rincon Hill and the newer Mission Bay neighbourhood. Housing costs are among the highest in the U.S. Check for rent-controlled units in older buildings, which follow different rules from newer construction.

Doing business downtown

Technology and artificial intelligence startups, finance and professional services dominate. With higher office vacancy than before 2020, landlords and the city have offered more flexible deals for pop-ups, restaurants and creative tenants — a window for independent operators willing to bet on the recovery.

Local tips

  • Ride the California Street cable car line for shorter queues than Powell Street.
  • Climb the Filbert Steps to Coit Tower through hidden gardens.
  • Check weekend events on Market Street, which is car-free downtown.

Questions people ask

What is the best area to stay in downtown San Francisco?

It depends on your plans. Financial District is the most central choice: towers around Montgomery and California Streets, including the pyramid-shaped icon of the skyline. For a different feel, consider Union Square — department stores, hotels and the cable car turnaround at Powell and Market.

How do you get around downtown San Francisco?

BART runs under Market Street to SFO and across the bay to Oakland and Berkeley. Walking is the best way to explore the core itself.

When is the best time to visit San Francisco?

Down.Town's pick is September–October for the warmest weather. Signature events include Bay to Breakers (May).

Is downtown San Francisco a good place to live?

Residential options include SoMa lofts, high-rise apartments in Rincon Hill and the newer Mission Bay neighbourhood. It scores 9/10 for walkability, 9/10 for transit and 2/10 for value in the Down.Town Index.
