In 30 seconds

  • Best for: food lovers and urban hikers who like steep streets and bay views
  • The Ferry Building is the best single stop for local food producers
  • Microclimates are real — carry a layer even in summer

Downtown San Francisco packs the Financial District, Union Square's shopping blocks, Chinatown and the South of Market district into a few hilly square kilometres between Market Street and the bay. It is one of the most transit-rich centres in the United States, and almost everything a visitor wants — the waterfront, the cable cars, the big museums and the best views — is within a half-hour walk of Market Street.

The pandemic hit downtown offices hard, and the city has spent the years since repurposing space: pop-up shops in vacant storefronts, new events on Market Street and homes in former offices. Some blocks still feel quiet, especially at weekends, and the contrast between polished towers and struggling streets can be stark. For visitors, though, the classics remain — cable cars, the Ferry Building and views from every hilltop — and the waterfront has rarely looked better.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Ferry Building Marketplace. A restored ferry terminal now home to food producers and a well-known farmers' market.
  • Cable cars. The world's last manually operated cable car system, running from Powell and Market and California Street.
  • Chinatown. One of the oldest Chinatowns in North America, entered through the Dragon Gate on Grant Avenue.
  • Salesforce Park. A long rooftop park built on top of the downtown transit centre.
  • SFMOMA. The San Francisco Museum of Modern Art, with large free-to-enter ground-floor galleries.
  • Coit Tower. An Art Deco tower on Telegraph Hill with murals inside and views of the bay.
  • Cable Car Museum. A free museum inside the working cable car barn and powerhouse on Nob Hill, where you can watch the huge winding wheels pull the cables under the streets.
  • Exploratorium. A hands-on science museum on Pier 15, good for all ages and especially popular with families on wet or foggy days.

A perfect day downtown

Start at the Ferry Building — on a Saturday morning the farmers' market wraps around the building — and take breakfast to the benches facing the Bay Bridge. Walk north along the Embarcadero toward the piers, then turn inland and climb the Filbert Steps through hillside gardens to Coit Tower for the view across the bay.

Drop down into North Beach for a coffee, then walk through Chinatown along Grant Avenue and Stockton Street, where the produce stalls and bakeries serve locals rather than tourists. Ride a cable car from California Street over Nob Hill, or continue to Union Square and on into SoMa for SFMOMA and the gardens and public art of Yerba Buena.

In the late afternoon, take the escalators or lifts up to Salesforce Park for a walk among the treetops before it closes for the evening. For dinner, choose between the ambitious restaurants of SoMa, the dim sum and banquet halls of Chinatown, or North Beach's Italian trattorias, and finish with a nightcap in one of North Beach's historic bars.

Eat and drink

San Francisco built much of modern American food culture around local produce, and the Ferry Building is its showroom. Chinatown and nearby North Beach offer dim sum and Italian cafés within walking distance of the Financial District. SoMa hosts many of the city's ambitious restaurants. Signature tastes include tangy sourdough bread, cioppino — a tomato-based seafood stew created by the city's Italian fishermen — and Dungeness crab in winter once the season opens.

To eat well for less, follow the office lunch crowd to counter-service spots in the Financial District on weekdays, or graze through Chinatown's bakeries and dumpling counters. Many downtown lunch places close at weekends, when the Ferry Building, Chinatown and North Beach are better bets. A short BART ride to the Mission District will reward you with the city's famous oversized burritos and some of its best-value eating.

Getting around

BART runs under Market Street to SFO and across the bay to Oakland and Berkeley, with a connector train to Oakland's airport. Muni Metro, buses, historic streetcars on the F-line and the cable cars cover the city, and the Central Subway's T Third line links Chinatown and Union Square with SoMa, the Caltrain station and Mission Bay. Ferries leave the Ferry Building for Sausalito, Oakland and other bay towns. Bay Wheels bike share is widely available. Caltrain's electric trains run from Fourth and King Streets down the Peninsula to Silicon Valley and San Jose.

Walking is the best way to explore the core itself, but the hills are real: Nob Hill, Telegraph Hill and Russian Hill are steep enough to plan around, while Market Street, the Embarcadero and SoMa are flat. Market Street is closed to private cars through much of downtown, which speeds up buses and streetcars. Driving is slow, parking is scarce and costly, and when you do park on a hill, turn your wheels into the kerb — it is the law and it is enforced.

Where to stay

Union Square is the most central base for shopping, theatres and cable cars, though its western edge borders the Tenderloin, so check exactly where a hotel sits. Nob Hill has grand old hotels, quieter streets and cable cars at the door, at the cost of a steep walk home. The Financial District and Embarcadero suit business travellers and anyone who wants the waterfront close by, though they are quiet at weekends. SoMa and Yerba Buena are best for conventions at the Moscone Center and the museums. Families often prefer the waterfront north toward Fisherman's Wharf, which trades local character for easy sightseeing.

Living downtown

Residential options include SoMa lofts, high-rise apartments in Rincon Hill and the newer Mission Bay neighbourhood. Housing costs are among the highest in the U.S. Check for rent-controlled units in older buildings, which follow different rules from newer construction. Downtown living suits people who want to walk to work, ride BART or Caltrain to the Peninsula and East Bay, and live without a car.

Leases usually run for 12 months, with credit and income checks at application, and California limits security deposits to one month's rent for most landlords. The city's rent-control rules cover most apartments in buildings completed before mid-1979, capping annual increases and adding eviction protections; newer towers are generally exempt from the city's cap, though a statewide limit can apply once a building is more than 15 years old. Parking, storage and in-unit laundry are often extras. Families are fewer downtown, but Mission Bay's parks and newer buildings have attracted some.

Doing business downtown

Technology and artificial intelligence startups, finance and professional services dominate. With higher office vacancy than before 2020, landlords and the city have offered more flexible deals for pop-ups, restaurants and creative tenants — a window for independent operators willing to bet on the recovery.

The Financial District remains the address for banks, law firms and investment managers; the newer towers around the transit centre house large tech tenants; and Jackson Square's historic buildings appeal to design studios and investors. SoMa and Mid-Market hold many of the city's tech companies in converted warehouses and newer offices, while Mission Bay is a hub for life sciences around a major university medical campus. Union Square's retail has lost several big flagship stores since 2020, opening space for experiential shops and food concepts. Every business in the city must register with the Treasurer and Tax Collector. See Office and commercial space in downtown San Francisco.

Seasons and weather

San Francisco's seasons rarely match expectations. Summer is often cool and foggy, especially in the mornings and evenings, when wind off the Pacific pushes the marine layer through the Golden Gate. September and October are usually the warmest, clearest months. Winter, roughly November to March, is the rainy season, with mild temperatures and storms that pass through in a day or two. Spring brings sunshine but also brisk afternoon winds. Downtown is often sunnier than the western neighbourhoods, but temperatures can drop sharply in an hour. Pack layers, a windproof jacket and comfortable shoes for the hills, whatever the month.

Safety and practicalities

Most visits are trouble-free, but a few things are worth knowing. The Tenderloin, between Union Square and Civic Center, and stretches of Mid-Market and Sixth Street have open drug use and visible homelessness; walk with purpose, stick to busier streets at night and plan routes around them if you prefer. Car break-ins are the most common crime affecting visitors, and phone snatching happens on busy streets, so keep valuables out of sight. BART stops running around midnight, while Muni's overnight Owl buses keep running. Cable cars are not wheelchair accessible, but BART and Muni Metro stations have lifts. California's earthquake alerts reach phones automatically; if you feel shaking, drop, cover and hold on.

Local tips

  • Ride the California Street cable car line for shorter queues than Powell Street.
  • Climb the Filbert Steps to Coit Tower through hidden gardens.
  • Check weekend events on Market Street, which is car-free downtown.
  • Visit the Ferry Building on a Saturday morning for the biggest farmers' market of the week.
  • Take a commuter ferry to Sausalito or Oakland for a bay cruise at a fraction of the cost of a sightseeing boat.
  • Pay with Clipper or a contactless card on every system, including the cable cars, to avoid fumbling for change.
