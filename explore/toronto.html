Downtown Toronto

Canada's business capital, stacked with condo towers, underground walkways and a lake ferry to the islands.

Downtown Toronto runs from the lakeshore up toward Bloor Street, and within that span it packs the country's financial centre, an entertainment district, two major universities' worth of students and one of North America's densest clusters of residential towers.

The city works at street level and underground. The PATH network of walkways connects office towers, malls and transit beneath the Financial District, while streetcars and the subway carry people above. In summer, the island ferries and the waterfront trail pull everyone back toward the lake.

Landmarks worth your time

  • CN Tower. The city's defining landmark, with observation levels and a glass floor.
  • St. Lawrence Market. A historic food market known for its peameal bacon sandwiches and Saturday farmers' market.
  • Nathan Phillips Square. City Hall's plaza, with the illuminated Toronto sign and a winter skating rink.
  • Toronto Islands. A short ferry ride to beaches, bike paths and the best skyline view in the city.
  • Art Gallery of Ontario. A major collection in a building reworked by architect Frank Gehry, on the edge of Chinatown.
  • Distillery District. Pedestrian streets of restored Victorian industrial buildings, with shops and a winter market.

Eat and drink

Toronto eats globally: Chinatown and Kensington Market sit on the western edge of downtown, Little Italy and Koreatown are a streetcar ride away, and the core has everything from food courts to chef-driven rooms. The city's restaurant promotion weeks offer fixed-price menus that make high-end dining more accessible.

Getting around

The TTC's Line 1 forms a U through downtown, and streetcars run along King, Queen and other east–west streets. The UP Express connects Union Station with Pearson Airport, and GO Transit trains and buses fan out to the region from Union. Bike Share Toronto has hundreds of stations, and much of the core is walkable year-round thanks to the PATH.

Living downtown

Condominium towers dominate housing; many rentals are individually owned condo units, so lease terms and quality vary by landlord. Neighbourhoods like St. Lawrence and CityPlace offer different trade-offs between space, noise and price. Factor in condo fees and parking costs if you buy.

Doing business downtown

Bay Street is Canada's financial centre, home to the major banks and the stock exchange. Tech, media and professional services fill the King West and Liberty Village offices. Street retail is strongest along Queen West and Yonge Street, where pedestrian volumes are among the highest in the country.

Signature events

SeptemberToronto International Film Festival — A major film festival that fills the Entertainment District with premieres.
Early OctoberNuit Blanche — An overnight festival of contemporary art installations across the city.

Local tips

  • Buy a PRESTO card or use contactless payment — fares cap per trip with free transfers within two hours.
  • Take the ferry to Ward's Island in the late afternoon for the skyline at sunset.
  • Use the PATH map apps on winter days; the network is large and easy to get turned around in.

Questions people ask

What is the best area to stay in downtown Toronto?

It depends on your plans. Financial District is the most central choice: bank towers around King and Bay, linked underground by the PATH. For a different feel, consider Entertainment District — theatres, the film festival's home cinema and a dense nightlife strip on King Street West.

How do you get around downtown Toronto?

The TTC's Line 1 forms a U through downtown, and streetcars run along King, Queen and other east–west streets. Walking is the best way to explore the core itself.

When is the best time to visit Toronto?

Down.Town's pick is May–October. Signature events include Pride Toronto (June).

Is downtown Toronto a good place to live?

Condominium towers dominate housing; many rentals are individually owned condo units, so lease terms and quality vary by landlord. It scores 9/10 for walkability, 8/10 for transit and 4/10 for value in the Down.Town Index.
