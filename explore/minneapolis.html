In 30 seconds

  • Best for: theatre and art lovers, cyclists and anyone who wants big-city culture with river and lake access
  • Use the skyways in winter; the "SK" button in lifts takes you to skyway level
  • Visit May to September, or come prepared for serious cold

Downtown Minneapolis rises on the west bank of the Mississippi at St. Anthony Falls, the only major natural waterfall on the river, which powered the flour mills and sawmills the city was built on. Today the core is a grid of office towers around Nicollet Mall, a street closed to private cars that runs south from the river, all stitched together by a second-storey skyway network. The old milling district by the falls has become the Mill District of museums, theatre and riverside parks; the North Loop, the former warehouse district to the north-west, is now the city's liveliest neighbourhood for food and shopping; and Downtown East has grown up around the stadium and the Commons park.

What makes Minneapolis work is the combination of serious culture, from the Guthrie and the Walker to a cluster of historic theatres and Orchestra Hall, with outdoor life on its doorstep: riverfront trails, the Chain of Lakes a short ride away and a park system locals guard fiercely. The skyways keep the weekday core functioning through long winters, but they also pull life off the street, so parts of downtown feel quiet in the evenings and at weekends outside the North Loop, Hennepin Avenue and event nights. Like most US downtowns, it is still adjusting to hybrid work, and its recovery since 2020 has been uneven.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Stone Arch Bridge. An 1883 stone railway bridge, now for walkers and cyclists, with views of St. Anthony Falls and the skyline; loop back through St. Anthony Main and over the Hennepin Avenue Bridge.
  • Mill City Museum. Built into the ruins of the Washburn A Mill, which exploded in 1878; it tells the story of the flour-milling city and has a rooftop observation deck.
  • Guthrie Theater. A midnight-blue theatre complex by the river. Even without tickets, you can walk out onto its cantilevered Endless Bridge for river views during opening hours.
  • Walker Art Center and Minneapolis Sculpture Garden. A leading contemporary art museum beside a free sculpture garden, home of the much-photographed Spoonbridge and Cherry.
  • Minneapolis Institute of Art. An encyclopedic collection, from ancient art to Asian galleries and period rooms, with free general admission, a short bus ride south of downtown.
  • Basilica of Saint Mary. A Beaux-Arts church on the edge of Loring Park, designated the first basilica in the United States.
  • Nicollet Mall. The city's main street, closed to private cars, with Orchestra Hall, Peavey Plaza, public art and the route of the city's big parades.
  • Minneapolis Central Library. A light-filled glass building by Cesar Pelli in the heart of downtown, worth a look for its atrium and a warm place to pause on cold days.

A perfect day downtown

Morning: walk to the river and start at Mill City Museum's observation deck to get the lie of the land, then cross the Stone Arch Bridge, wander St. Anthony Main and return over the Hennepin Avenue Bridge. On summer Saturdays the Mill City Farmers Market sets up beside the Guthrie; in winter, warm up with a skyway walk through the core instead.

Afternoon: walk through Loring Park to the Walker Art Center and the Sculpture Garden, or ride a bus south to the free Minneapolis Institute of Art. In summer, rent a bike and loop the Chain of Lakes, Bde Maka Ska, Lake of the Isles and Lake Harriet, on paved paths a few miles south-west of downtown.

Evening: eat in the North Loop, then catch a show at the Guthrie, Orchestra Hall or one of the restored theatres on Hennepin Avenue. On game nights, the crowds head for Target Field, Target Center or U.S. Bank Stadium, all within walking distance of downtown hotels.

Eat and drink

Minnesota's food traditions start with its lakes, prairies and immigrant communities. Look for walleye, a mild freshwater fish served fried or in sandwiches; wild rice, traditionally harvested by Ojibwe and Dakota people, in soups and salads; and the Juicy Lucy, a burger with molten cheese sealed inside the patty, which two rival south Minneapolis bars both claim to have invented. Scandinavian and German heritage shows up in baking, holiday lefse and the church-supper hotdish. Just as important are newer communities: Somali restaurants in Cedar-Riverside, Mexican and Ecuadorian cooking along Lake Street, Vietnamese and pan-Asian kitchens on the stretch of Nicollet Avenue known as Eat Street, and Hmong markets across the river in St. Paul. The city has also become a showcase for modern Indigenous cooking.

Craft beer took off after a 2011 state law allowed breweries to open taprooms, and there are now dozens across the city, many in the North Loop and Northeast. For good-value eating, use weekday happy hours, the food counters along the skyways at lunch (most close by early evening), Eat Street's pho shops and the markets and taquerias of Lake Street. The Mill City Farmers Market runs on Saturday mornings from spring to autumn, with an indoor winter market on selected dates, and the larger Minneapolis Farmers Market on Lyndale Avenue, just west of the North Loop, is a summer institution for produce and flowers. Patio season is short and treasured: on the first warm evening, every terrace in the North Loop fills.

Getting around

Metro Transit runs the region's buses and light rail. The METRO Blue Line runs from Target Field Station through downtown and Downtown East to both terminals at Minneapolis–Saint Paul International Airport and on to Bloomington; the Green Line runs east from the same station through the University of Minnesota to downtown St. Paul. A growing network of rapid bus lines, plus frequent buses on the Marquette and 2nd Avenue transit corridors, links downtown with the rest of the city. Buy a Go-To Card, use the Metro Transit app or pay at a platform ticket machine, and keep proof of payment, since light rail fares are checked on board.

Downtown's grid is easy to walk, and the skyways connect much of the core at second-floor level: useful on a January morning, confusing on your first visit, and largely closed in the evenings and at weekends. Minneapolis is a serious cycling city, with protected bike lanes downtown, the Cedar Lake Trail and the Midtown Greenway, and paths along the river and lakes; a bike-share system runs in the warmer months. Driving is straightforward outside rush hour, and parking ramps, as Minnesotans call garages, are plentiful, though prices climb on game nights. In winter, watch for declared snow emergencies, when parking rules change street by street and cars that are not moved are ticketed and towed.

Where to stay

For winter visits or conventions, stay in the downtown core near Nicollet Mall, where many hotels connect to the skyway and the Minneapolis Convention Center is a short walk away. The North Loop is the pick for food lovers and design fans, with converted warehouses, independent shops and light rail at Target Field Station. The Mill District and riverfront suit walkers and theatre-goers, with the Stone Arch Bridge and the Guthrie outside the door. For a game at U.S. Bank Stadium, Downtown East is closest. Visiting the University of Minnesota or St. Paul? The Green Line makes it easy to stay downtown and ride across, though St. Paul's own downtown is a calmer alternative.

Living downtown

Housing downtown ranges from North Loop warehouse lofts and new mid-rise apartments to riverfront condos in the Mill District, towers in Downtown East and older brick walk-ups around Loring Park and Elliot Park. Across the river, Northeast and St. Anthony Main offer more of a neighbourhood feel with quick bus links. Compared with Chicago or the coasts, Minneapolis offers good value for a city of its cultural depth, but property taxes and winter heating bills are real costs, and heated underground parking is a feature worth paying for.

Apartment leases usually run 12 months and require a security deposit. Minneapolis licenses rental properties and has its own rules on tenant screening and deposits, and Minnesota law requires landlords to return deposits, or explain deductions, within three weeks of the end of a tenancy. Check what is included, since heat, internet and parking are often billed separately, and ask whether the building connects to the skyway. Downtown suits young professionals, empty-nesters downsizing from the suburbs and car-light households who value walking to work, theatre and the river. Families more often choose the lake neighbourhoods of south-west Minneapolis or the inner suburbs for space and schools.

Doing business downtown

For a metro of its size, the Twin Cities host an unusually large group of Fortune 500 headquarters, and several of them, in retail, banking, energy and financial services, anchor towers in downtown Minneapolis. The Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis sits on the riverfront, the city and Hennepin County run their governments from downtown, and law, accounting, consulting and advertising firms fill the skyway-linked towers around Nicollet Mall and Marquette Avenue. The wider region is known for medical technology and health care, the cluster nicknamed Medical Alley, and for food and agribusiness, though most of those headquarters sit in the suburbs.

Office space divides into the skyway-connected towers of the core, newer buildings in Downtown East and the warehouse conversions and new timber-frame offices of the North Loop, which draw creative and technology firms. Retail follows the skyways and the weekday office crowd in the core, while the North Loop's Washington Avenue and the Hennepin Avenue theatre strip carry evenings and weekends. Restaurants downtown should budget for the extra local taxes on food and liquor that apply in the city's downtown taxing area. Signature events

Mid-MayArt-A-Whirl — Artists across the Northeast Minneapolis Arts District open their studios to the public for a long weekend.
Late JuneTwin Cities Pride — A big, free Pride festival in Loring Park, with a parade along Hennepin Avenue.
JulyMinneapolis Aquatennial — A summer festival of river, lake and street events, including the Torchlight Parade on Nicollet Mall.
Early OctoberTwin Cities Marathon — A marathon from downtown Minneapolis past the lakes and along the river to the State Capitol in St. Paul.

Dates move from year to year. Check the organiser's official site before booking travel.

Seasons and weather

Winter, from December to early March, is long, cold and snowy, with regular spells below 0°F (−18°C) and wind chills that make exposed skin sting. The city handles it well, with ploughed streets, skyways that keep people moving and residents who embrace skating, cross-country skiing and frozen-lake festivals, but you need a proper insulated coat, boots with grip, a hat, gloves and a scarf. Spring arrives late and fast; April can bring snow one week and patio weather the next. Summer, from June to August, is warm and often humid, with thunderstorms, long evenings and everyone outdoors; bring sunscreen and insect repellent for the lakes. September and early October are crisp and colourful, often the most pleasant weeks of the year, though the first frosts come early.

Safety and practicalities

Downtown Minneapolis is busy and generally safe during the working day, but its rhythms matter: with offices emptying in the evening and skyways closing, some streets feel deserted at night and at weekends. Hennepin Avenue's bar strip can be rowdy around closing time on weekend nights, so stay with the crowds or take a taxi. Light rail is well used and patrolled by transit police and safety staff, but stay alert late at night and keep valuables out of sight; car break-ins happen here as in any US city. The real hazards are seasonal: frostbite and icy pavements in winter, heat and storms in summer. The skyways, light rail and most venues are step-free, mobile coverage is strong, and skyway maps are posted throughout the system.

Local tips

  • In lifts, the "SK" button takes you to skyway level.
  • Skyways mostly close in the evening and at weekends; check building hours before relying on them.
  • Hire a bike in summer and ride the river and lake paths; the Grand Rounds parkways loop the whole city.
  • If you have a car, watch for snow emergency announcements; towing is swift.
  • Big shows at the Guthrie and the Hennepin Avenue theatres sell out, so book ahead.
  • Take the Blue Line to the airport; it stops at both terminals.
