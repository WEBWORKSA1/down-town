In 30 seconds

  • Best for: warm-weather city living with a Latin American flavour
  • Ride the free Metromover for an elevated tour of downtown and Brickell
  • Hurricane season runs June–November

Downtown Miami and neighbouring Brickell form a forest of residential and office towers on Biscayne Bay. In the last two decades, the area has become a true live-work district, with tens of thousands of residents, rooftop bars and waterfront parks. The Miami River divides the two: Brickell's banks and condo towers lie to the south, while the older downtown core of Flagler Street, the courthouses, the arena and the bayfront museums spreads to the north.

The city's Latin American ties shape everything from the language you hear in shops to the banks lining Brickell Avenue. North of downtown, Wynwood's murals and the Design District's boutiques give the city its creative edge. Miami Beach sits across the bay on its own barrier island, linked by causeways, which is why downtown feels more like a business and residential centre than a beach resort.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Pérez Art Museum Miami. A bayfront museum with hanging gardens on its terraces.
  • Bayfront Park. A downtown park that hosts concerts and festivals.
  • Wynwood Walls. An outdoor museum of large-scale murals.
  • Freedom Tower. A historic tower linked to the Cuban exile experience.
  • Frost Museum of Science. A science museum and aquarium in Maurice A. Ferré Park (formerly Museum Park), next door to the art museum.
  • Calle Ocho. The main street of Little Havana.
  • Adrienne Arsht Center for the Performing Arts. The city's main stage for opera, ballet, Broadway tours and concerts, in two large halls on Biscayne Boulevard.
  • Miami Circle. A ring of ancient post holes cut into the bedrock at the mouth of the Miami River, a National Historic Landmark that recalls the Tequesta people who lived here long before the city.

A perfect day downtown

Start the way Miami does, with a Cuban coffee from a walk-up window, then spend the cooler morning hours at the Pérez Art Museum and the Frost Museum of Science in Maurice A. Ferré Park. Walk south along the bay through Bayfront Park, past the Freedom Tower, and pick up the Metromover for a free elevated ride into Brickell.

In the afternoon, walk the riverfront to the Miami Circle, then take a short ride west to Little Havana. Stroll Calle Ocho, watch the domino players in Máximo Gómez Park and stop for a guava pastry. Later, head north to Wynwood as the light softens and the murals glow.

For the evening, go back to Brickell for a rooftop drink as the towers light up across the bay, then dinner in Brickell or downtown. If there is a show at the Arsht Center or a game at the arena, end the night there — or ride the Metromover's Brickell loop for a last look at the skyline.

Eat and drink

Cuban coffee and sandwiches, Peruvian ceviche, Venezuelan arepas and fine-dining rooms in Brickell all compete for attention. Rooftop bars are a big part of the evening scene.

The essential local ritual is the ventanita, a walk-up café window where you order a colada — a small cup of sweet, strong Cuban coffee meant to be shared in thimble-sized servings — alongside croquetas and pastelitos. Little Havana is the heartland, but the city's food also reflects Colombia, Argentina, Nicaragua, Haiti and the wider Caribbean. In season, from mid-October into spring, stone crab claws are the classic splurge, and key lime pie is the default dessert. To eat well for less, stick to ventanitas, lunch specials and food halls, and always read the bill: many restaurants add a service charge automatically, so you may not need to tip again.

Getting around

The free Metromover loops through downtown and Brickell. Metrorail connects to Miami International Airport, and Brightline trains run from downtown to Fort Lauderdale, West Palm Beach and Orlando. Citi Bike Miami adds options.

The Metromover has three loops — the downtown inner loop and branches to Brickell and to the Omni area near Edgewater — and Metrorail's Orange Line runs from Government Center station to the airport. Tri-Rail commuter trains to Broward and Palm Beach counties also reach the downtown rail station, and the City of Miami runs free trolley routes through several neighbourhoods. Driving is common but stressful: I-95 and the causeways to Miami Beach clog at rush hour, drawbridges over the Miami River open for boats, and garage parking in Brickell is expensive. Walking is easy in winter, but in summer plan short walks for mornings and evenings.

Where to stay

Brickell suits business travellers and anyone who wants restaurants, rooftop bars and the Metromover on the doorstep. Downtown and Bayfront put you near the museums, the arena, the performing arts centre and PortMiami, which makes it the natural choice before or after a cruise. Edgewater is quieter, with bay views and quick access to Wynwood and the Design District. If the beach is your priority, stay on Miami Beach and visit downtown by bus or car. Rooms book out and prices jump during Miami Art Week, Ultra and the winter high season, so reserve early; and check whether a condo-style rental is allowed by the building before you book.

Living downtown

High-rise condos dominate. Florida has no state personal income tax, but insurance costs for property and cars are high and rising. Check hurricane-proofing and flood risk before you sign.

Many downtown and Brickell rentals are individual condo units owned by investors rather than purpose-built rental buildings, so expect to apply to both the owner and the condo association, which may charge its own application fee and set rules on pets, moving times and lease length. Leases usually run for 12 months. Florida sets no cap on security deposits but strictly regulates how they are held and returned. Since the 2021 Surfside condominium collapse, the state requires structural milestone inspections and stronger reserves for older condo buildings, which has pushed up association fees and, in turn, some rents. Downtown suits professionals, international newcomers and empty-nesters who want walkable towers with pools and bay views; families often prefer Coral Gables, Coconut Grove or the suburbs.

Doing business downtown

Finance, international trade, law, crypto and tech relocations, and tourism drive the area. Spanish-language service is a commercial advantage for many businesses.

Brickell Avenue is home to international and private banks, wealth managers and law firms with Latin American practices, while the downtown core clusters government offices, courts and the firms that serve them. PortMiami, one of the world's busiest cruise ports, and the airport's cargo trade make logistics and travel major employers, and Wynwood has emerged as a base for creative and tech firms in newer mid-rise offices. Retail ranges from luxury in the Design District to Brickell's malls and restaurant rows and Flagler Street's older shopfronts. Seasons and weather

Miami has two seasons. The dry season, roughly November to April, brings warm, sunny days, lower humidity and the biggest crowds. The wet season, May to October, is hot and humid, with short, heavy thunderstorms most afternoons, and overlaps with the hurricane season. In autumn, seasonal high "king tides" can flood low-lying streets even on sunny days. Pack light, breathable clothing, strong sunscreen, sunglasses and a compact umbrella, plus a layer for powerful indoor air-conditioning.

Safety and practicalities

Brickell and the bayfront are busy and generally comfortable, but some downtown blocks away from Biscayne Boulevard empty out after office hours, so take a rideshare at night rather than walking long distances. Do not leave valuables visible in parked cars. Never drive or wade through flooded streets, and in summer seek shelter when lightning starts. If a hurricane threatens, follow Miami-Dade County's official alerts and your airline's and hotel's guidance, and do not wait until the last minute to leave. The Metromover and Metrorail are step-free, though lifts can be out of service. Heat and sun are the main everyday hazards: hydrate, wear a hat and rest indoors at midday. Some Spanish helps, but English works everywhere.

Local tips

  • Ride the Metromover's Brickell loop at night for city views.
  • Visit Wynwood in the morning for photos without the crowds.
  • Ask landlords about flood history and windstorm insurance.
  • Order a colada from a ventanita and share it — that is the point.
  • Check restaurant bills for an automatic service charge before adding a tip.
  • In summer, schedule outdoor plans for the morning; afternoon storms arrive almost daily.
