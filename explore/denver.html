In 30 seconds

  • Best for: outdoor-loving professionals who want easy mountain access
  • Take the train from the airport to Union Station
  • At altitude, drink more water and go easy on alcohol at first

Denver's downtown sits on a grid at the meeting point of the South Platte River and Cherry Creek, with the Rocky Mountains on the western horizon. Lower Downtown, known as LoDo, is the historic heart: brick warehouses turned into restaurants, lofts and galleries around a beautifully restored Union Station. The central business district's towers rise to the south-east, and the gold dome of the State Capitol marks the far end of downtown, where the city's museums and civic buildings gather.

The 16th Street corridor connects LoDo with the Civic Center and the cultural district, while the River North Art District just to the north has become one of the city's most creative neighbourhoods. What makes downtown work is that rare combination of a walkable, transit-served centre with mountains an hour or so away: people work, eat and watch baseball downtown during the week and head for trails and ski slopes at the weekend. The trade-offs are a core that still has more empty offices than before 2020, visible homelessness on some blocks and a cost of living that has climbed fast.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Union Station. A restored 1914 rail hall that is now a hotel, bar and transport hub; its Great Hall is one of the city's favourite meeting places.
  • Larimer Square. The city's oldest block, strung with lights above restaurants and shops.
  • Coors Field. The baseball stadium on the edge of LoDo, with views of the mountains from the upper decks.
  • Denver Art Museum. A major art museum with an angular titanium-clad wing; the Clyfford Still Museum is next door and History Colorado Center a short walk away.
  • Colorado State Capitol. A gold-domed capitol with a step marking an elevation of one mile, and a sweeping view west to the Rockies from its steps.
  • Confluence Park. A riverside park where the creek meets the river, popular for summer tubing and the start of trails along both waterways.
  • Denver Performing Arts Complex. Theatres and concert halls for Broadway tours, opera, ballet and the symphony, gathered under a glass-canopied galleria.
  • Civic Center Park. A formal park with a Greek-style amphitheatre between the Capitol and the City and County Building, home to festivals and the holiday market.

A perfect day downtown

Morning: start at Union Station, where the Great Hall is a fine place for coffee, then cross the Millennium Bridge over the rail tracks to Commons Park and follow the South Platte to Confluence Park. Walk up into Lower Highland for brunch with a skyline view, or loop back to Larimer Square, the city's oldest block.

Afternoon: walk or ride the free shuttle along 16th Street to Civic Center and spend the afternoon in the Golden Triangle: the Denver Art Museum, the Clyfford Still Museum next door and History Colorado Center nearby. Finish on the west steps of the State Capitol for the mountain view and the mile-high marker.

Evening: in summer, catch a game at Coors Field and stay for LoDo's bars; otherwise, see a show at the Denver Performing Arts Complex, or head to RiNo for murals, breweries and food halls. Summer evenings often bring a thunderstorm rolling off the mountains, so keep a covered patio in mind.

Eat and drink

Denver's dining scene has grown fast, with chef-driven restaurants in LoDo and RiNo, and craft breweries everywhere. Mexican and Southwestern flavours are local strengths. Green chile is the dish to try: a stew of roasted green chiles, often with pork, ladled over burritos ("smothered") or eaten by the bowl, with fierce loyalty to chiles from Pueblo, south of the city. Colorado lamb, bison, mountain trout, Palisade peaches in late summer and Olathe sweet corn appear on menus in season, and the curious can try Rocky Mountain oysters, a ranch-country delicacy that is not seafood. Federal Boulevard, west of downtown, is the go-to strip for Vietnamese pho and Mexican taquerias.

Craft beer is a point of civic pride: the Great American Beer Festival is held here each autumn, and brewery taprooms cluster in RiNo, LoDo and along the river. Food halls in RiNo and at Union Station make grazing easy, happy hours are generous, and the dry climate means patio season stretches from spring to the first autumn storms. To eat well for less, go for happy hour, lunch specials and the taquerias and noodle shops off the main tourist streets. Remember that alcohol hits harder at altitude, so drink water alongside.

Getting around

RTD runs light rail and commuter rail; the A Line connects Union Station with the airport. Denver International sits far out on the plains north-east of the city, so the train is often quicker than a taxi at rush hour, though airport trips carry a higher fare than local rides. Union Station is the hub of the network: commuter rail lines run north and west to the suburbs, light rail lines skirt downtown on their way south towards the Denver Tech Center and west towards Lakewood, and an underground bus concourse handles regional buses, including the express service to Boulder. Pay with an RTD MyRide card or the mobile app.

Downtown is walkable, and bike lanes and scooters link the surrounding neighbourhoods. The rebuilt 16th Street reopened in 2025 after a multi-year reconstruction, and RTD's free 16th Street FreeRide shuttle (formerly the Free MallRide) runs its length between Union Station and Civic Center Station; check RTD for current downtown shuttle service and hours. The Cherry Creek Trail and the South Platte River Trail give cyclists traffic-free routes out of downtown. Driving is easy outside rush hour, but I-25 and I-70 into the mountains jam on weekend mornings, especially in ski season, and downtown parking prices rise on game and concert nights.

Where to stay

LoDo and the streets around Union Station are the best all-round base: historic character, restaurants and bars, Coors Field and a direct train to the airport. The 16th Street corridor and the central business district put you close to the Colorado Convention Center and the Denver Performing Arts Complex, and suit business travellers. The Golden Triangle is quieter and best for museum-goers, while RiNo, with its own A Line stop at 38th and Blake, suits travellers who want breweries, galleries and street art. Cherry Creek, a few miles south-east, is the polished shopping-district alternative. Book early for convention weeks, big concerts and the Great American Beer Festival, when rooms fill quickly.

Living downtown

LoDo lofts and new apartments near Union Station appeal to young professionals. Housing is cheaper than coastal cities but has risen steeply over the past decade. Beyond LoDo, Capitol Hill has the city's densest mix of older brick apartment buildings and grand Victorian houses divided into flats, Five Points and RiNo have filled with new mid-rise buildings, and Lower Highland offers townhouses with skyline views across the river.

Leases typically run 12 months with a security deposit. Colorado law bars cities from imposing rent control, but recent state laws have strengthened tenant protections, including a requirement for landlords to give a qualifying reason for most evictions, and Denver requires residential landlords to hold a rental licence. Check whether parking, storage and utilities are included, since many new buildings charge for them separately. Downtown suits young professionals and outdoor lovers who want a short walk to work, bars and ballgames during the week and a quick getaway to the mountains at weekends; families tend to choose neighbourhoods such as Washington Park or Highland, or the suburbs, for space and schools.

Doing business downtown

Energy, aerospace, telecom, outdoor-industry brands and tech fill downtown offices. The traditional office core runs along 17th Street, once nicknamed the "Wall Street of the Rockies", where banks, law firms and energy companies occupy towers built during the oil booms of the 1970s and early 1980s. Since Union Station's redevelopment, newer towers have risen around the station and in the Central Platte Valley, drawing technology, finance and professional-services tenants, while LoDo's brick warehouses and RiNo's newer buildings attract creative firms. State and city government around the Capitol and Civic Center add a steady base of public-sector work.

Retail and hospitality depend heavily on sports crowds and conventions at the Colorado Convention Center. 16th Street, Larimer Square and the blocks around Coors Field are the main shopping and dining streets, with RiNo's breweries and food halls the busiest newer corridor. Competition from the Denver Tech Center and Cherry Creek is real, and, like most US downtowns, Denver's core has more vacant office space than it did before 2020. Seasons and weather

Denver's climate is semi-arid and sunny, with big swings between day and night. Summer, from June to August, is warm and dry, with frequent afternoon thunderstorms, occasional hail and cooler evenings; it is peak season for festivals, baseball and mountain trips. Autumn brings clear, crisp days and golden aspens in the mountains from late September. Winter alternates sunny, mild spells with snowstorms, but snow in the city usually melts within days, and some of the heaviest snow often falls in March and April. Spring is the most changeable season, with T-shirt days and blizzards in the same week. Pack layers, a warm jacket from October to April, sunglasses, sunscreen, lip balm for the dry air and a reusable water bottle.

Safety and practicalities

Altitude is the first practical issue: at a mile high, alcohol hits harder, the sun burns faster and the dry air dehydrates you, so pace yourself on the first day. Downtown is generally safe and busy, but homelessness and drug use are visible in places, including parts of 16th Street, Civic Center and around transit hubs; stay aware, especially late at night. Car break-ins are common, so leave nothing on show. LoDo gets crowded and rowdy around bar closing time at weekends and after games. Light rail and commuter rail are generally safe, with security staff, but quieter late at night. If you head into the mountains, check the weather and road conditions first, since storms can close passes. Light rail, the airport train and most venues are step-free, and mobile coverage is strong downtown.

Local tips

  • Start at Union Station and walk to Larimer Square.
  • Day-trip to the mountains early to avoid traffic.
  • Wear sunscreen — the altitude makes UV stronger.
  • Use the free 16th Street shuttle to hop between Union Station and Civic Center.
  • Stand on the State Capitol's west steps for the mile-high marker and a view across to the Rockies.
  • Book summer concerts at Red Rocks Amphitheatre, the city-owned venue in the foothills, well ahead.
