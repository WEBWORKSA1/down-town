In 30 seconds

  • Best for: civil-rights history, big-ticket sport and conventions, and Southern food in a leafy, sprawling city
  • Ride MARTA from the airport: the station sits right at the domestic terminal
  • Check the full street name — dozens of Atlanta roads are called some version of Peachtree

Atlanta's downtown is less a single centre than a spine. Downtown proper, around Five Points and Centennial Olympic Park, holds the state capitol, Georgia State University, the convention district and the stadiums. A couple of miles north along Peachtree Street, Midtown has the newest office towers, the arts centre, Georgia Tech and Piedmont Park, and further north again Buckhead forms a third business district. A sunken interstate, the Downtown Connector, slices between them and the older neighbourhoods to the east.

That shape explains Atlanta's strengths and frustrations. It grew around the railway and then the motorway, so distances are larger and pavements emptier than in older East Coast cities, and summer heat discourages long walks. But the MARTA rail spine ties the airport, Downtown and Midtown together, the BeltLine — a loop of trails and parks on old rail corridors — has made the inner neighbourhoods walkable again, and the tree canopy earns Atlanta its nickname as a city in a forest. Few American cities pack so much civil-rights history into a short walk.

Landmarks worth your time

  • Martin Luther King Jr. National Historical Park. The visitor centre, the historic Ebenezer Baptist Church sanctuary and the King Center, where Dr King and Coretta Scott King are buried; check the National Park Service for birth-home tour availability.
  • National Center for Civil and Human Rights. An affecting museum linking the American civil-rights movement to human-rights struggles worldwide, with an unforgettable lunch-counter simulation.
  • Centennial Olympic Park. The Olympic legacy park, with the Fountain of Rings that children run through in summer.
  • Georgia Aquarium. One of the largest aquariums in the world, best known for whale sharks in its huge ocean tank; book a timed entry and go early.
  • Georgia State Capitol. A 19th-century capitol whose dome is covered in gold leaf mined in north Georgia; free self-guided visits on weekdays.
  • High Museum of Art. The South-East's leading art museum, in gleaming white buildings on Peachtree Street in Midtown.
  • Fox Theatre. A lavish 1920s movie palace with Moorish and Egyptian decoration and a starlit ceiling, now a busy concert and Broadway venue.
  • Piedmont Park and the Eastside Trail. Midtown's great lawn, lake and dog parks, linked to the BeltLine's most popular walking and cycling trail.

A perfect day downtown

Begin in the King district while it is cool: the visitor centre, Ebenezer Baptist Church and the reflecting pool at the King Center take a couple of unhurried hours. Have breakfast or an early lunch at the Sweet Auburn Curb Market, a municipal market in business for about a century, then ride the Atlanta Streetcar or walk west to Centennial Olympic Park.

Spend the afternoon at the National Center for Civil and Human Rights or the Georgia Aquarium, which sit side by side on the park. Then take MARTA north to Arts Center station for the High Museum and a wander through Midtown, finishing at Piedmont Park as the light softens.

In the evening, walk or bike the Eastside Trail south from the park towards the Old Fourth Ward and Inman Park, where converted warehouses hold food halls, patios and bars. If you have tickets, end the night with a show at the Fox Theatre or a game downtown.

Eat and drink

Atlanta is one of the best places in the country to eat Southern food at every level. Look for meat-and-three counters (a main plus three sides such as collard greens, macaroni cheese and candied yams), fried chicken, shrimp and grits, biscuits, peach cobbler and pitchers of sweet tea. Lemon-pepper wings are a local obsession, and Sunday brunch is a serious social occasion. The Sweet Auburn Curb Market downtown is a good place to graze cheaply.

The most exciting food in the region often sits outside downtown. Buford Highway, a drive or rideshare north-east of Midtown, is a long corridor of Korean, Vietnamese, Chinese, Mexican and other immigrant-run restaurants that rewards an empty stomach. Closer in, the converted industrial buildings along the BeltLine house food halls and patios that suit groups who cannot agree on what to eat. To eat well for less, go to food halls at lunchtime and use weekday happy hours in Midtown.

Getting around

MARTA runs the rail and bus network. The Red and Gold lines run north–south from the airport through Downtown and Midtown to the northern suburbs, with the Red Line serving Buckhead, the Blue and Green lines run east–west, and all four meet at Five Points. The airport station sits at the end of the domestic terminal, so you can step off a plane and onto a train to Downtown or Midtown without a taxi. The Atlanta Streetcar loops through Downtown to the King district. Trains run from early morning until around 1 a.m., and fares can be paid with a Breeze card or app, or by tapping a contactless bank card or phone.

Walking works within Downtown and within Midtown, and the BeltLine's Eastside Trail is the best car-free link to the eastern neighbourhoods; shared bikes and e-scooters are widely available. Beyond that, Atlanta is a driving city. The interstates are congested at rush hour, and game days and big conventions bring heavy traffic and road closures around the stadium and arena. Downtown has plenty of parking decks, but leave nothing visible in a parked car.

Where to stay

Midtown is the most rounded base: museums, the park, restaurants and nightlife are all close, and three MARTA stations put Downtown and the airport within easy reach. Downtown, around Centennial Olympic Park and Peachtree Center, makes most sense for conventions, games, concerts and families visiting the aquarium, but it feels empty late at night. Buckhead suits shopping trips and business travellers with meetings there. For a neighbourhood feel, short-term rentals in the Old Fourth Ward and Inman Park put you on the Eastside Trail — check that the host is properly registered. For an early flight, the hotels around the airport are a practical last night.

Living downtown

Midtown's high-rise apartments and condos, the lofts of Castleberry Hill and Downtown's converted office buildings, and the mid-rise blocks along the BeltLine give Atlanta a broad choice of urban housing. Leases usually run for 12 months, with application fees and background checks standard. Georgia sets no statutory cap on security deposits, so amounts vary, but most professional landlords must give you a written list of existing damage before taking one. Many apartment buildings charge separately for parking, which matters here more than in most downtowns.

Downtown and Midtown suit young professionals, students and empty-nesters who want walkable restaurants, culture and a short MARTA ride to work or the airport, at generally better value than the big coastal cities. Most residents still keep a car for errands, trips to the suburbs and anywhere off the rail spine. Families tend to look further out, or at the in-town neighbourhoods just east of the BeltLine.

Doing business downtown

Atlanta's business districts line up along Peachtree Street. Downtown carries government, Georgia State University, sports and a huge convention and hospitality trade; Midtown has the newest towers and the city's tech scene, built around Georgia Tech's Tech Square and the corporate innovation centres that cluster nearby; and Buckhead is the base for finance, law and wealth management. The metro area is home to a deep bench of corporate headquarters, and its strengths include logistics (thanks to the airport), payments and financial technology, film and television production, media, healthcare and public health — the federal Centers for Disease Control and Prevention is headquartered in Atlanta.

Retail is strongest where people already walk: Peachtree Street in Midtown, the shopfronts and patios along the Eastside Trail, and the galleries and bars of Castleberry Hill. Downtown retail is shaped by the event calendar — busy around games, concerts and conventions, quiet on other evenings. Signature events

AprilAtlanta Dogwood Festival — A long-running arts fair and spring festival under the blossoming trees of Piedmont Park.
Late MayAtlanta Jazz Festival — A free, city-run jazz festival in Piedmont Park over Memorial Day weekend.
Early JulyPeachtree Road Race — One of the world's largest 10K races, run down Peachtree from Buckhead to Midtown on Independence Day.
Labor Day weekendDragon Con — A vast fan convention that takes over the downtown hotels, with a costumed parade along Peachtree Street.

Dates move from year to year. Check the organiser's official site before booking travel.

Seasons and weather

Spring is lovely but comes with a catch: in late March and April, dogwoods and azaleas bloom and pine pollen coats cars and pavements yellow, so allergy sufferers should pack antihistamines. Summer is long, hot and humid, with frequent afternoon thunderstorms; plan outdoor sightseeing for mornings and carry water, a hat and a light rain layer. Autumn is the best season, with warm, dry days well into October and November. Winters are mostly mild, but an occasional ice storm or light snowfall can bring roads to a standstill, so keep plans flexible.

Safety and practicalities

Downtown and Midtown are busy and comfortable during the day and on event nights, but some Downtown blocks empty out after office hours, so stick to well-used streets after dark and take a rideshare for longer hops at night. Car break-ins are the most common problem for visitors: never leave bags, laptops or shopping visible in a parked car. Wide, fast roads make some crossings stressful, so use signals rather than dashing across. MARTA stations have lifts, though they are occasionally out of service, and the newer trails and parks are step-free. Heat is the main health risk in summer. Mobile coverage is reliable across the core.

Local tips

  • Take MARTA from the airport to Downtown or Midtown and skip the motorway traffic.
  • Always check the full street name — Peachtree Street, Peachtree Road, West Peachtree and Peachtree Center Avenue are all different places.
  • Walk or bike the Eastside Trail early in the morning or near sunset to avoid both heat and crowds.
  • On game, concert and convention days, ride MARTA rather than drive near the stadium and arena.
  • Book the aquarium and civil-rights museum together; they share a plaza and fill a day comfortably.
  • In spring, rinse pollen off your car before it rains and turns to yellow paste.
